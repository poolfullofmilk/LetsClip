# LetsClip

An instant replay recorder for one monitor or one game or app window that runs in the background, a lightweight Medal.tv in the owner's words. A WPF window hosts a `BlazorWebView` whose UI is MudBlazor, laid out like `SoTF.Editor`, with `WiFiWatch`'s tray and startup registration. It starts with Windows in the tray and keeps the last 30 seconds to 10 minutes of the chosen monitor (the primary until one is picked) or of a known game's or added app's window (`AFK-Assist`'s `GameScanner`), with system audio (or, with Only Record Game and Voice Chat, just the game's and the voice apps' sound) and a cleaned microphone, which also gets a track of its own beside the mix, in a rolling cache. A hotkey saves them as an MP4 clip of exactly that length, named after its game (`Overwatch 2026-11-14 21-05-33.mp4`), with a toast over a full screen game. The Clips page lists them by day, each tagged with its game, app or monitor, searches them, a menu on each card to rename, copy, show or delete it with its details nested, and filters them by game or to the unedited ones as each box is ticked; the editor names a clip (renaming its file), trims it to the frame over a strip with the sound's waveform, cuts it losslessly (Export and Save, with Restore Original) and exports an exact copy of the selection, with or without the game's sound or the microphone, or fits it under a Discord plan's limit as HEVC. Any clip copies to the clipboard as a file from its card's menu or the editor, whose main button copies instead of exporting whenever the export would only be the clip itself. Changing the clips folder moves everything with a progress bar. It installs itself as `LetsClip.exe` with an entry in Settings, Apps, checks for updates by itself, installs them in the background once it has sat hidden for a while and then says what changed. It runs on its own slim FFmpeg build, which converts a monitor's frames on AMD and NVIDIA graphics cards.

## Every Round, Start To Release

Do every step below, in order, every round, without being reminded: the owner should never have to repeat any of it. Be thorough and be sure. Measure rather than eyeball, look at every screen the round touched, and never call something done that was not seen working.

1. `git pull` first, since the owner edits the README on GitHub.
2. Read every file the change touches and its callers before editing. A problem the owner points out once is a pattern: search the whole app for the same control, wording, spacing or shadow and fix every place in the same round.
3. Build with `dotnet build LetsClip.slnx`: zero errors and zero warnings. A running sandbox locks `bin`, so stop it first.
4. Run every check: `dotnet format analyzers` and `dotnet format style` with `--verify-no-changes --severity info` on each of the three projects, `csharpier format .` then `csharpier check .`, `powershell -File ~/.claude/scripts/RazorStyle.ps1`, `git ls-files --eol` showing no `w/lf` and no `w/-text`, and the comment length check over the C#, `LetsClip.css` and `AppShell.js`. Normalising line endings with `sed` gives a file without a final newline a lone CR at its end, which git then reads as binary (`w/-text`): trim that byte.
5. Run a ponytail ultra audit over what the round touched and anything it grew: dead parameters and fields, single use wrappers, markup repeated where one shared component belongs (like `ChoiceSelect`), settings nobody needs. Apply what it finds and say what was cut.
6. Look at it in a sandbox: a Debug build with `LETSCLIP_ROOT` on a scratch folder, `TestClips` copied into its clips folder, a 30 MB noisy clip and a 110 MB 1440p one made with `testsrc2` and `noise` so every Discord plan has work, and settings whose `RemovedGames` holds any game that is running, so nothing records the owner's game. Seed its `Window` on the owner's second monitor (he plays on the first and watches the sandbox there), a third of that screen's height, exactly centred, then 8 px taken off every side, the owner's pick of 12 November 2026 since it looks cleaner (the top of the screen and the whole screen were tried and turned down): `"Window": { "Left": 2560, "Top": 336, "Right": 4000, "Bottom": 1153, "IsMaximized": false }` on the portrait 1440 x 2560 monitor (work area 2560, -510, 1440 x 2500), 846 px above and 845 below. The sides' 8 px is measured on the visible frame (`DwmGetWindowAttribute` 9): the window's minimum width there is the screen's own 1440 px, whose invisible resize borders leave 8 px each side, and a wider side margin pushes it off the right edge (40 px was tried). Seed it with `SaveSound` off and `PlayerMuted` on; a sandboxed window never takes focus. Recording the first monitor for a test is allowed.
   - Sweep over CDP at 1920 x 1009, 1600 x 845, 1440 x 760, 1366 x 705, 1280 x 680 and 1264 x 680 (the smallest page the window allows), on Clips, the filter popover, the editor with a landscape and a portrait clip, Settings, the Games and Apps dialog, the More menu and About. Measure each state: the page never scrolls sideways, nothing sits past its panel, no label or button wraps, no two children of a row overlap, panels never overlap each other (the stacked editor once drew Export over the clip), and every text cut off in dots is one meant to be (a long name, the sandbox path).
   - Take screenshots of every state at 1920 x 1009 and 1264 x 680 at least and look at each one, plus zooms at scale 4 to 6 of anything about alignment, shadows, radii or spacing.
   - Use every control the round touched for real: selects through real mouse presses, typed times with Enter and with leaving the field, drags, the filter, the delete question, Export and Save, Save Cut, Restore Original, hotkey picking and the games search. The page console stays free of errors.
   - A media change (capture, audio filters, exports) gets a measured test on FFmpeg's own sources before it ships, never a guess.
7. Update this file for everything that changed: what it does now, the owner's picks and what they replaced, and the numbers measured.
8. Release only once the owner said publish; saying so for the round, or asking for the whole publish flow, counts:
   1. `git pull` again.
   2. Publish to a scratch folder with `-p:BaseOutputPath=<folder>\`, then take the screenshots as under Screenshots from that exe copied alone into an empty folder, which is also the release's lone exe test.
   3. Update the README to match what shipped, one short plain bullet per feature.
   4. Bump `<Version>` to the next patch (`1.8.5` after `1.8.4`) unless the owner names another.
   5. Commit everything as one short Title Case line with no body and no attribution, publish again from that commit (the exe embeds it as `AppInfo.Commit`, which the next update's What Changed compares from), and push to `master`.
   6. Sign the exe: `openssl dgst -sha256 -sign ~/.letsclip/update-signing.pem <exe> | base64 -w0 > <signature file>` in Git Bash. Then `gh release create v<version> <exe> --title "LetsClip v<version>" --notes-file <signature file>`: the release body is that one line and nothing else. Check that `releases/latest` redirects to it, that the asset's `digest` matches the local exe and that the body verifies (`Docs/Release.md`, Signed Updates). An update without a valid signature is refused by every install from v2.0.4 on.
   7. Delete the previous release with its tag (`gh release delete <tag> --yes --cleanup-tag`) and the local tag (`git tag -d`).
   8. Install it the way every user gets it: restart the owner's copy through an `explorer.exe`-started `.cmd` with `--tray`, so it checks at once and updates itself from the release after 10 minutes hidden. Should that fail, stop it, wait 10 s and start the downloaded release exe through such a script, which installs it as `LetsClip.exe`. Have a script confirm that `LetsClip.exe` runs from the install folder, the Run value and shortcuts point at it, its SHA-256 matches the release and no `LetsClip_v*.exe` is left beside it.
   9. Clean up the install folder, the sandbox and any stray `ffmpeg.exe` from tests. Delete `C:\LetsClip` too: the safety check refused it on 13 November 2026 and allowed it on 14 November; if refused, ask the owner.
9. Report in a few lines what changed and anything left for the owner.

## Build, Run And Publish

The FFmpeg binaries are not in git. After cloning, once:

```bash
powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File LetsClip.Desktop/FFmpeg/Get-FFmpeg.ps1
```

```bash
dotnet build LetsClip.slnx
```

```bash
dotnet run --project LetsClip.Desktop
```

```bash
dotnet publish LetsClip.Desktop -c Release
```

The notes behind these, the screenshots and the update flow are in `Docs/Release.md`.

## Looks Wrong, Is Not

- Each capture writes two segment streams (`seg_` video, `aud_` audio); the save joins them by their shared timestamps.
- `ClipSaver` writes the clip as `.LetsClip-<guid>.mp4` and renames it only after it probes back; such leftovers after a crash are ignored by the library and never deleted, since the clips folder belongs to the user.
- `CacheFolders.Delete` refuses anything but a GUID folder directly under the cache root and only deletes files named like segments or x264 pass logs.
- The preview plays with sound while recording, so its sound lands in the next clip, the owner's pick since clips open unmuted (it used to start muted and warn on unmuting); with Only Record Game and Voice Chat it stays out, since WebView2 plays in LetsClip's own process tree. `letsClip.setVolume` sets the video's `muted` and `volume` itself: a `muted` attribute only mutes an element the HTML parser made, never one Blazor adds, so before v1.3 the preview played sound behind a muted icon.
- An export runs on after leaving the editor (`ExportRunner`), one at a time, with `Exporting 42%` centred in the app bar (`ExportChip`, a click opens its clip); another clip's Export waits with `Another clip is exporting`, and its clip cannot be deleted, cut or restored meanwhile. A finished export says so in a snackbar with Copy File unless its own editor is open (that shows the Exported panel), and in a tray balloon while the window is hidden. Export and Save cuts through the open editor's player when its clip is open, else directly. Exiting leaves a running export to the kill-on-close job, like a crash.
- `BlazorWebView.DisposeAsync` disposes its WebView2 after a `ConfigureAwait(false)`, usually on a pool thread, where WebView2's COM calls throw `InvalidCastException` and the controller never closes: every page release from 9 to 12 November failed that way and kept WebView2's memory. The page's template uses `PageWebView`, which moves that dispose back to the UI thread (released in 38 ms, all six WebView2 processes gone, measured 13 November 2026).
- A window capture initialises a second D3D11 device (`d3d11va=spare`) that nothing uses: with two, FFmpeg gives the encoder neither, so AMF makes its own. Sharing the capture's device crashed AMD's own driver thread (`amdxx64.dll` 32.0.32015.7029, a null read) when the window resized while AMF encoded, the cause of the owner's game window crashes (`Docs/Recovery.md`).
- A Discord plan export is HEVC (`hvc1` tag, Main profile) on the graphics card: `hevc_amf`, `hevc_nvenc` or `hevc_qsv` after a 12 frame test (`VideoEncoders.HevcOf`, `WorksAsync`), then that card's H.264, then x264 two pass, the owner's pick of 13 November 2026. Off stays an exact copy and capture stays H.264. HEVC scored 6 to 12 VMAF over H.264 at the same size, and 1080p beat 720p and 540p at every bitrate from 0.8 to 5 Mbps, so an encode keeps 1080p down to 0.012 bits per 1080p pixel and frame (1.5 Mbps at 60 FPS, where AMF's HEVC still lands on its bitrate; it overshoots by 14 to 29% below), 720p down to 0.005, 540p under that (`SizeBudget`). The verdict thresholds stay H.264's, so a selection HEVC could still carry may read Too Long. Discord plays HEVC on desktop and web since 1 July 2024 and about 95% of browsers decode it (caniuse); a device without an HEVC decoder sees a download box, the file still plays in VLC. The progress line still reads `Encoding on AMD AMF`; the log names the codec (`As HEVC With AMD AMF`).
- Replacing a clip (a cut, a restore) retries five times a second apart (`ClipLibrary.ReplaceWhileBusyAsync`): a picture pass or a card's preview reading it lets go in a moment.
- The tray balloon can open another clip into the open editor: each loaded clip has its own lifetime (`_lifetime` token, its own `DotNetObjectReference` let go on detach, its pending trim written when it ends), so a probe or timeline strip still running for the last clip never lands on the next, and `ExportPanel` is keyed by the clip's path, so an export or a pick never carries over.
- A saved clip holds up to a second of keyframe pre-roll below zero behind its MP4 edit list: `ffprobe -show_packets` lists it with negative times and the discard flag, players never show it.
- A lossless cut or Off export of a clip with B frames (`has_b_frames` 2, a file copied in from another recorder, or the x264 test clips) keeps frames past End: a stream copy ends on decode order, and the MP4 edit list then shows the frames whose times run past it (470 frames for a 7.8 s cut of a 60 FPS clip against 468, measured on 9 November 2026, its first 468 matching exactly; up to 7 frames for the x264 `veryslow` test clips with 8 B frames, a 54 s cut coming out 54.234 s on 12 November 2026). LetsClip's own recordings never have B frames (`-bf 0`), so their cuts are frame exact, and an export for a Discord plan re-encodes and is exact either way.
- `ExtendedLimits` (`FFmpeg`) and `AdapterDescription` (`MonitorCatalog`) are explicit layouts holding only the fields read, at their 64 bit offsets, like `ModeInfo`; the app is x64 only. Checked after the change: children die with a killed parent and the AMD adapter is still found.
- A capture's FFmpeg is disposed only after its exit code is read (`SegmentProcess.Exited` always completes), so `ReplayRecorder.WatchEndAsync` never waits forever; `GameWatcher` reads and lets go of the found game's handle under one lock.
- A still game window's clip can end its video before its audio: the last frame holds while the sound runs on to the press.
- A warm-up clip's file starts with about a second of audio before the first video frame, and that first frame can show content older than its timestamp (`ddagrab` probes it at start); thumbnails come from a second in.
- The video FFmpeg holds about 600 MB of private memory, flat over a 10 minute run; the audio one about 23 MB.
- Opening the monitor list shows a `MonitorOverlay` on every monitor (accent edge and name on the picked one, grey on the others), and a pick stays outlined 1.5 s. They are placed and drawn at zero opacity, then all made visible in one dispatcher pass, since shown one by one the second monitor lit up a moment after the first. The overlays are click through, never activate and carry `WDA_EXCLUDEFROMCAPTURE`, so a screen grab or a clip never shows them even while buffering.
- The playhead draws above the trim handles at full height, so it never thins to half its width at the start or end; it is as wide as the grip (`--grip-width`) and its colour (`--grip`), centred on its time.
- The trim handles sit outside the kept part, their inner edge on the time, so two handles never cross or overlap; the timeline pads both ends by a handle's width (`--handle-width`) and maps time over the inner `timeline-track`. The strip runs the timeline's whole width, under the handles too, and the outer shades stretch the same handle width, so no grey end shows without a picture; Every part of the frame is a whole number of screen pixels at any Windows scale, the owner's rule that it looks the same at every width: `--device-pixel` (one screen pixel in CSS pixels, set by `AppShell.js` from `devicePixelRatio` and renewed through a narrow `matchMedia` resolution range, since the ratio is rarely an exact decimal) sizes the grip's gaps, width and height, the bars and the handles with `round()`, and positions round to it too (`percent` in `AppShell.js`, `ClipEditor.Percent`), so all parts share one sub-pixel phase and snap alike. At 125% the grip measured 6 | 4 | 6 screen pixels across at every position, where whole CSS pixels gave a grip of 3 or 4, and 26 | 28 | 26 down since it grew to 22 px (28 | 22 | 28 at 18 px); emulated across, 8 | 5 | 8 at 150%, 9 | 5 | 9 at 175%, 10 | 6 | 10 at 200% and 5 | 3 | 5 at 100%. The frame is padding and a spread, never borders: Chromium floors border widths to screen pixels, so an inexact ratio such as 1.0000000149 would lose one. CDP's emulation re-evaluates media queries only when an override starts or ends, and its screenshots of a scale it emulates are resampled, so check a scale through a cleared override and real pixels at the window's own scale. Dragging and arrow keys run in `AppShell.js`: it reads the range from the handles' `data-time`, paints handles, shades and selection itself, seeks the video, and tells Blazor once (`OnRange`) when a drag ends or a key steps, so no frame waits on a render. Blazor's next render matches what the script painted.
- Play runs from the playhead and stops at the end point, starting over from the start point once there.
- The timeline strip decodes keyframes only, one a second, into 40 cells 144 px high (`ClipLibrary.StripCells`, twice the timeline's height), sampled over the duration less one second. The page draws it on a canvas (`letsClip.showStrip`): as many whole frames as fit at the clip's own shape, each the moment under its middle between the handles, again on every resize. A portrait clip shows 25 upright tiles where 12 were stretched three times as wide. A copied clip with sparse keyframes (OBS, x264's default 250 frames) runs out early, so `tpad` repeats the last one and no tile stays black.
- A trim time typed with Enter keeps the field focused: the page script writes where the point landed (`place`), so `7` reads `7s`, a typo puts the last time back and `99` reads the clip's end, ready for the next fix. Tab or a click away still commits the same way.
- Thumbnails appear one by one: the picture pass raises `PictureMade` per clip and the Clips page swaps that card's picture in place; one clip that fails leaves a plain tile and the pass goes on.
- A clip with no metadata reads its recording time from its generated name (`ClipNames.DateOf`), so a copied file keeps its date.
- WebView2's browser keys, zoom and pinch zoom are off (`AreBrowserAcceleratorKeysEnabled`, `IsZoomControlEnabled`, `IsPinchZoomEnabled`).
- AMD's AMF runtime prints onnxruntime's CPU core lines on standard error before anything else, with any FFmpeg build. The capture's error reader skips lines that do not start with `[`, and `FFmpeg.RunAsync` keeps only the last error line, so they never become a reason.
- A cut clip's file is shorter than its hidden original and its write time is new, so a cut clip sorts by its recorded date (metadata), never by the file's time.
- An Off selection's Bitrate is the clip's own, less any sound track the export leaves out, whatever the selection, the owner's word of 14 November 2026 (`whatever makes the most sense`). Weighed over its length it climbed as the selection shortened, the owner's question of 13 November: a lossless cut starts at the keyframe before Cut Start, so a short copy carries up to a second of frames the player never shows (on a 29 Mbps clip 20 s read 30.2 Mbps and 0.5 s 86.8), while the frames are the same. Size still weighs the copy exactly, so a short selection's Size can read more than its length times that bitrate.
- The editor's main button reads Copy File when the export would only be the clip itself (Off, its sound kept, the whole clip) and Save and Copy for a shorter selection, the owner's ask of 13 November 2026: the cut is saved like Save Cut (asking first while originals are deleted), then the clip is copied, so no identical file lands in Exports. Muting the sound or picking a plan the clip is over brings Export back.
- The Clips filter applies each tick at once through the cards' fade, yet its boxes show a copy (`_draft`): the cards change inside a view transition, and boxes bound to the filter itself would flick back until it lands.
- A nested `MudMenu` draws MudBlazor's filled `ArrowRight` with no parameter to change it, so `LetsClip.css` redraws that path as the rounded one (`d: path(...)`).
- Reset to Default keeps the clips folder, the games list and what the pages remember (sort, player volume, window, the export's plan and sound, the last update's commit): `UserSettings.WithDefaults` names what stays, so a new setting resets by itself.
- A clip recorded with its microphone apart (cleaned or at a volume other than 100%) holds three sound tracks, the mix, the game and the microphone; players, Discord and Explorer play only the first, marked default, and an export keeps the one its Game Audio and Microphone rows pick (`Docs/Capture.md`, Three Sound Tracks).
- Only a monitor recorded upright at its own size converts on the GPU (AMF and NVENC, `scale_d3d11`); any scaling and every window stay on the CPU route, whose Lanczos is sharper than the GPU's scaler (`Docs/Capture.md`).
- The in-game toast never shows in a screenshot or a clip: it carries `WDA_EXCLUDEFROMCAPTURE` like the monitor overlays, so a test can only check its window, never its look (`Docs/Verify.md`).
- A clip's file is renamed with its name, so its details are keyed again under the new path (`ClipLibrary.MoveDetails`); a file renamed in Explorer instead reads as a clip without details, its date from the stamp in its name.
