# LetsClip, Text And Layout

Every wording, spacing, colour, radius and shadow rule the owner picked. Read before touching any page, component, the stylesheet or the page script. Part of `CLAUDE.md`, its rules bind the same way.

## Text

- Labels are short Title Case, and inside one the short joining words stay lower case, the owner's pick: a, an, the, and, or, as, at, by, for, in, is, of, on, per, to, with (Start with Windows, Show in Folder, LetsClip v2.0.1 is Out; `is` joined the list on 9 November 2026, when a capital Is read as not clean). The first and last word keep their capital. Comments keep every word capitalised, RazorStyle checks that.
- Longer sentences (descriptions, hints, warnings, snackbars) are sentence case, short, and never end in a symbol; with two sentences only the last goes without a full stop.
- The info icon (inlined in `Clips.razor`, its one use, in that audit; the glyph is `AppTheme.InfoIcon` everywhere) is a grey `Notes` icon like every other icon button, the owner's pick after light blue read as odd (`AppTheme.InfoIcon`, the owner's pick over Material's circled i, used everywhere an info icon appears: cards, the About item, Info Notices and Info snackbars) in an icon button's 26 px box whose content opens in a tooltip on hover, left of it so a card at the window's edge never squeezes it; it holds a clip card's Resolution, Bitrate and Size (`Clips.FactsOf`, `info-facts`). Explanations are not captions: a row that needs one is renamed until it does not, the owner removed Size Limit's.
- Every alert is a `Notice` (dense, raised, rounded, warnings in their own colour), and CSS sets its text to the body2 size of the text around it. Like the capture panel's button below it, the owner's pick, its 20 px icon sits 16 px from the left edge, centred on the button's icon, and its text is centred in the same width (`text-wrap: balance`, so two lines read evenly). The export's hint and its result name centre too.
- A value about a choice sits greyed (`mud-text-secondary`) on the right where the row has room, never in brackets: a monitor's size and refresh rate in its select item, a device's adapter beside its name, Recommended beside the recommended capture choice, the export size. Every item is an `ItemLabel` (its text never gives way, the detail ends in dots), and the closed select shows the same detail and colour through `FieldDetail` (a greyed `data-detail` on the right, the text amber or red like its item), so a closed field reads like its open item. Devices are named the Windows way, the owner's pick after `Intel® Smart Sound Technologie voor digitale microfoons` stood in for a microphone: the endpoint name (Microphone Array, Headphones, Luidsprekers) with the adapter greyed beside it, trademark marks taken out (`AudioDevices.NamesOf`, with a self test).
- Both audio selects start with Automatic (`AudioDevices.AutomaticId`, an empty id that follows the Windows default device, for the microphone too), then a line (`divided` on that item: a `MudDivider` inside a `MudSelect` also renders under the field), then the devices. A Rounded check (`ItemLabel.Checked`, a hidden one keeps every name in line) marks the picked item and, while Automatic is picked, the device it follows too, the owner's pick, so Automatic carries no greyed device name, in the list or the closed field. A saved device that is gone reads Automatic and records through the default (`AudioMixer` falls back, also when the device is pulled out mid session) until it returns, then takes over again; the saved id stays. Both selects and the Noise Cancellation switch always show, disabled while their switch is off, never hidden. Test Microphone (left of the microphone select) opens the picked microphone, or the default, in `MicrophoneMeter` and draws its level along the bottom of the select (`LevelMeter`, its own 20 renders a second). It is live, the owner's pick over a first version that recorded five seconds and played them back: the microphone goes through an FFmpeg running the filters a clip gets (`CaptureSession.VoiceCleaning`, only with Noise Cancellation on) at the volume a clip opens at (`UserSettings.OpeningVolume`) straight to the Windows output, until Stop Test, leaving the page, turning the microphone off or a lost device, which ends it with a Notice (`Ended`). Changing the device or Noise Cancellation restarts it at once. The system audio loopback is muted for its whole length, renewed each second so a session that restarts stays muted, so no clip holds the test. A snackbar says headphones avoid an echo, since on speakers it can feed back. Measured on the real filter chain: speech comes out about 110 ms after it goes in (63 ms without cleaning, FFmpeg's low delay flags changed nothing), plus 20 ms of microphone and 30 ms of output buffer. Clock drift cannot build up delay: past 120 ms buffered it catches up (`s_maximumLag`). On `aevalsrc` speech at -35 dB over pink noise at -48 dB the word comes out at -40.9 dB and the gap at -65.5 dB with cleaning, -40.7 and -53.6 dB without, the same at 44.1 kHz, and the first second is as clean as the rest. Three seconds of pure silence say so, since on the owner's i5 a microphone that was not muted in Windows sent only zeros (-113 dB), which is what Windows' privacy setting or a mute key does. A switch whose list is empty cannot be turned on, only off.
- Settings and panel rows are a title and their control, no description under them (the owner's pick); the capture and export panels use the same `SettingRow`, whose `Switch`, `SwitchChanged` and `SwitchDisabled` put a switch at the row's end, named by the row's title. A row's title never gives way (`flex-shrink-0`) and pushes the rest right with an auto margin, so a field that should fill the row (Name) can; its value does give way, in dots, so at 300 px the capture panel reads Game or App beside Waiting, never `Game or A…`. The status chip in the app bar is a plain chip, no tooltip or click, and names the game it records (Recording Overwatch). Every panel holds its content 24 px from its edge (`px-6`), cards 16 px (`pa-4`).
- No full path on screen: the clips folder shows relative to the profile (`Videos\LetsClip`), whole only when it lies outside it, and the empty Clips page says only No Clips. Its icon is grey like No Games Found in the Games and Apps dialog (`EmptyState` defaults to grey), the owner's pick over blue.
- No app is named: exports are ready to share and copied files paste anywhere. The one exception is About, which shows the GitHub repository description word for word, the owner's pick (since 9 November 2026 `Keeps the last moments of your game, app or monitor in the background, saves them with a hotkey and exports clips small enough for Discord`, where it said one monitor; the GitHub description must read the same), beside the version, the author as `Poolfullofmilk` with a capital P (`AppInfo.Author`, the owner's pick; the csproj's `Company` keeps the account name) and one Third Party Notices button.
- The monitor overlay names a monitor as `Display 1: 1920 × 1080` (`MonitorInfo.Label`), the same words the logs use.
- Numbers follow the Windows format (`Formatter` uses the current culture). Every shown date goes through `Formatter.FormatDateTime`, the owner's pick: `Today at 8:30` and `Yesterday at 12:16`, older ones `6-11-2026 at 8:42` (day and month without leading zeros, 24 hour time without one). Every shown time and length goes through `Formatter.FormatDuration`, zero parts left out (`8m30,5s`, `1m`, `0s`), the player clock and replay lengths too. A whole number never shows `,0`, the owner's rule for every number: `30 Mbps`, `20 MB`, `2 GB`, beside `30,5 Mbps` and `1,25 GB` (`FormatSize` with `0.#` and `0.##`, `FormatBitrate` with `0.#`, `formatSize` in the page script alike). Saved clips keep the sortable `yyyy-MM-dd HH-mm-ss` file name, which the library reads the date back from; an export of one is named by its full date, never Today, with `h` for the colon a file name cannot hold (`LetsClip 6-11-2026 at 8h42`, `Formatter.FormatFileDate`). A clip's title is its given name, else its game or app, else the monitor it recorded by its place in the monitor list, Monitor 1 or Monitor 2, the owner's pick (`ClipMetadata.Monitor`, set by `CaptureController.SaveAsync`; older clips read Monitor) (`ClipInfo.Title`, `DefaultTitle`, and `Source`, the word the game filter uses), and a copied file without a generated name shows its file name; its date and size follow on one row, on the cards too.
- The capture panel's links say what they do, the owner's pick on 9 November 2026 over the earlier questions (Record a Monitor Instead?): Switch to Monitor Recording and Add a Game or App (the Games and Apps dialog, `GamesDialog.ShowAsync`) stand under the Game or App row at the panel's full width, the title beside Waiting, so they never wrap at the 300 px panel (`game-row`, a grid whose links span both columns; beside the title they broke after Monitor), and Switch to Game and App Recording stands under the monitor select. To look at monitor mode in a sandbox without recording, rename the build's `FFmpeg\ffmpeg.exe` first, so it stops on Recording Error. The Save button names the hotkey on a second line (or F10), hidden while the hotkey is not registered. The Capture header holds Use Recommended Settings (`AutoFixHigh`), which sets resolution, frame rate and quality to the recommended ones in one change, so capture restarts once; it is disabled while all three already are.
- Closing the window hides it to the tray with no balloon, the owner's pick on 9 November 2026: a one time `LetsClip Is Still Running` balloon went, with its `HasSeenTrayHint` setting.
- A hotkey another app holds is said in a balloon at startup too, since a tray start shows no panel. The Save Hotkey row is one button showing the keys, the owner's pick: a click pauses the hotkey and reads Waiting for Keys, every press replaces the keys shown (a slip is simply pressed again, an unusable one says why), and OK or Enter keeps them, Cancel or Escape puts the old ones back (`letsClip.watchKeys`); while picking, Cancel (a red text button) and OK (filled, disabled until the keys are usable) sit left of the keys button. Hiding the window or leaving the page cancels too (`letsClip.stopKeys`), so the saved hotkey always comes back and no key is swallowed later. The Clips Folder row is Use Default Folder (shown only while a folder is picked), a filled grey button showing the folder that opens it, and Change. Disk Space under it reads `34 MB Used · 760,59 GB Free`, every MP4 in that folder, exports and kept originals included (`ClipLibrary.SpaceOf`). Delete Originals When Cutting follows, on by default, the owner's pick (`UserSettings.DeleteOriginals`): Save Cut and Export and Save then replace the clip with the cut and keep no full copy, dropping any original an earlier cut kept, so no history is left; off, the first cut keeps the full clip in `.Originals` for Restore Original. Kept Originals shows their size or None, with a red filled Delete Kept Originals button that asks first (`YesNoDialog.ConfirmAsync`), listing the cut clips that lose their way back by title with their recorded date greyed on the right, in the Games and Apps dialog's outlined table (`YesNoDialog.Clips`, a long title ends in dots, `title-cell`), the owner's pick on 9 November 2026, and deletes every file in `.Originals` (`ClipLibrary.DeleteOriginals`, a file in use stays for the next delete); the cut clips stay as they are. A Discord plan only re-encodes the export, so the clip always keeps its cut at full quality (checked: a Free Export and Save gave a 1920 x 1080 export of 17.5 MB and left the clip a 2560 x 1440 cut). While originals are not kept, Save Cut and Export and Save ask first, the owner's pick (`YesNoDialog.ConfirmCutAsync`, titled by the button): The parts outside the selection are deleted forever, with Yes in red and a Keep Originals in Settings link under it that closes the question and opens Settings on Storage (`Settings.StorageRoute`, `settings#storage`, `Panel.Id`). Settings shows Storage once its devices and sizes have loaded, since they move it down (`letsClip.reveal`, `scrollIntoView` with `block: nearest`, never animated), and a panel scrolled into view keeps the page's 28 px bottom gap (`scroll-margin-bottom`). Kept originals cut with no question. The old original's delete after such a cut only logs if it fails (`ClipLibrary.DeleteOriginal`), since the cut itself already landed.

## Layout

- The editor fills the window and ends `--page-bottom` above it (`page-fill`, `fill`, `fill-column`); a dialog table scrolls inside its rounded box (`.mud-table.fit` with a `max-height`). The window is at least 1280 wide (`MinWidth`, the owner's pick, the page then 1264 px) wherever the screen allows; on a screen narrower than that in effective pixels (a 1080p laptop at 175%, a 1440 wide portrait monitor at 125%) `MainWindow.FitToScreen` lowers the minimum to the work area and zooms the page (`ZoomFactor`), so the same layout just gets smaller and nothing sits off screen, rechecked when the window moves or its DPI changes, so the side panel always sits beside the page; the stacked layout below 1100 px and the icon only navigation below 900 px went with it.
- The clip fills its player edge to edge, the same 24 px from the panel's sides as from its top, the owner's pick, as long as that leaves the controls row room to centre Play: the side panel's width is what the clip or the controls leave, whichever is narrower (`.split.editor` is a size container, `100cqw` less the larger of the clip's width at the height left over, `--clip-aspect` from the probe, and `--controls-min-width`), between 300 and 480 px. `--controls-min-width` is both trim fields mirrored on each side of the transport (`4 × --trim-field-width + 3 × --action-size + 6 × 8 px`, 621.5 px). Without it Play sat 24.5 px right of centre with a landscape clip at 1280 and 1264 x 680 and 87 px with a portrait one; now it is centred at every sweep size, and a landscape clip gets 1.8 px of panel beside it at 1366 x 705 and 24.5 px at 1280 and 1264 x 680, the one place it no longer fills (decided on 9 November 2026, a centred transport over the fill). It counts on the rows under the player keeping fixed heights (`--controls-height` 66 px: the 40 px line, a 6 px gap and the 20 px clock; `--timeline-height` 72 px). At 1920 x 1009 the side panel is 370 px and a landscape clip fills its 1158 px box; a portrait clip or a very wide window gets the widest side panel and the clip centred. The side panel scrolls like a page (its margins and padding reach into the gutters, its scrollbar sits at the window's edge, `--side-content` is the width inside), so its panels keep their width while it scrolls and their shadows are never clipped.
- Clip cards sit straight on the page, never inside a panel, filling rows in a CSS grid of 272 px columns or wider (`clip-grid`, 12 px apart), so the stacked chips always fit: a `MudGrid` went three wide at 1280 px and 107 px tall thumbnails. Card actions always show, never only on hover. Each thumbnail carries two filled dark chips, the owner's pick: the date bottom left and the length bottom right; the resolution, the bitrate (the file's size over its length) and the size moved from chips into the info icon, labelled there, since a bare `22,3 Mbps` chip read as unclear. Right of the info icon sits Copy File (`ContentCopy`, grey), then the red bin, 30 px apart (measured at 1920 and 1264 wide). Copy File puts the clip itself on the clipboard like Explorer's Copy, so it pastes into Discord or a folder without an export, which for an uncut clip only made an identical second file (`DesktopShell.CopyFile`, which every copy shares with its snackbar: Copied, paste it anywhere with Ctrl+V, the clipboard is busy, or the file was moved or deleted). The title sits under the thumbnail as an `h6` beside those three, one line. No card render touches the disk: a thumbnail's URL carries no stamp, since a picture is named by its clip's size and write time and never changes in place, and a clip's URL is stamped with `ClipInfo.WrittenUtc`, read once when the library lists it (before, every card read its thumbnail's and clip's write times on every render, a filter keystroke included). Thumbnails load lazily (`loading="lazy"`, `decoding="async"`). Resting anywhere on a card for 250 ms, its title row too but never its info icon or bin, plays the clip muted and looped over its thumbnail, the owner's pick (`clip-preview` holds a `<video>` that `AppShell.js` adds and removes, so Blazor never sees it), with a red 4 px progress bar on a dark track, with the picture shadow (`--picture-shadow`, the owner asked for more depth), inside the frame under the chips and in line with them, exactly centred in the 8 px between the date chip and the picture's bottom edge (2 px either side, measured). Leaving, a press, hiding the window or the card going away ends it and lets go of the file; a pointer just passing over loads nothing, and Windows' reduce animations turns it off. A clip recorded in Games and Apps Only keeps its game (`ClipMetadata.Game`, from `CaptureOptions.GameName`), which titles its card unless it has a name; no game chip sits on the thumbnail, the owner's pick, since the title already says it.
- The Clips header holds Filter by Game or App (`FilterList`, a blue badge dot while a filter is on): a `MudPopover` under it with a search field, an All checkbox and one checkbox per game, Monitor 1 and Monitor 2 for monitor clips (Monitor for older ones), matches marked by `MudHighlighter` (`mark` reads tonal, as in SoTF.Editor), Reset and Apply in its footer. It edits a copy until Apply; every game checked means no filter, so a new game shows. The filter lasts while LetsClip runs (`Clips.s_shownGames`) and the count reads `2 of 4`; a filter that hides everything offers Show All Clips. Sort Clips, left of the filter, is a menu of Newest, Oldest, Largest and Longest First (`UserSettings.ClipSort`, a blue badge dot unless Newest); sorting and filtering slide the cards through `letsClip.transition`. The last list stays in `Clips.s_clips`, so coming back from another page shows the cards at once with no spinner, then quietly refreshes. On Clips and Settings only the content scrolls: the header with its buttons, the capture panel and the navigation stay put, the owner's pick (`Header Scrolls`, `page-scroll` taking the page's gutters as its padding, `page-fill` on the page).
- The editor's header shows the clip's title like every page header (`header-title`, live as the name is typed). The side panel is one Details panel, the owner's pick on 9 November 2026 over Details above Export: Name and Recorded (the date greyed), then the export's choices, Fit for Discord and Audio, then what the export gets, Length, Size, Resolution, Frame Rate and Bitrate, then Export with Save Cut beside it. A finished export opens an Exported panel under it, titled and padded like it, the owner's pick (`ExportPanel` renders both, Name and Recorded come in as its `ChildContent`): a File row, then Copy File at full height with Show in Folder as a grey square beside it, and the side panel scrolls just enough to show it (`letsClip.reveal("exported")`), its bottom then level with the main panel's. With Exported shown the side panel scrolls at 1440 x 760 (53 px), 1366 x 705 (67 px) and 1280 and 1264 x 680 (93 px), left so on 9 November 2026 since the reveal brings the result into view; Details alone still fits every sweep size. Name is a field exactly as wide as its text, with no minimum (`content-field`, `field-sizing: content`, right aligned, a long name ends in dots at the row's edge), holding the title the header shows, Monitor 1 or the game when no name was given, the owner's pick: clearing it or typing that default back stores no name (`ClipEditor.TypeTitle`), and the default stands as its placeholder while it is empty. The panel is `compact`: every row exactly 40 px, the 40 px fields too, and no lines between them. Below 720 px of window height the rows without a field drop to 35 px, so the export's rows, Fit for Discord among them, never scroll the side panel (it overflowed 29 px at 1264 x 680; measured clean at 1920, 1600, 1366 and 1264 wide). Typing saves 400 ms after the last key (with the trim's timer), leaving or Enter or Escape saves at once. The name lives only in its metadata (`ClipMetadata.Title`, `ClipInfo.CustomTitle`): the MP4 keeps its sortable name, the owner's pick, and exports are named by it (`ClipNames.FileSafe`). Pending writes land before a cut, a restore, another clip or leaving.
- Settings cards fill rows in reading order, Recording and Saving, then Audio and Storage (`settings-grid`, the owner's pick), two wide once each column gets 656 px (the Microphone row needs 640: title, Test Microphone, the 400 px select and the switch), so from a window about 1660 px wide; each card keeps its own height.
- Every page puts 8 px between its header row and its content (`Header`, and the editor's own row), panels 24 px apart; dialog content and actions keep 24 px from the edges. Content that scrolls beside a header ends exactly on the header's right edge: the 10 px scrollbar draws 12 device pixels at 125%, 9.6 px, so the gutters subtract `--scrollbar-width` (`round(down, 10px, --device-pixel)`); Clips, Settings and the side panel ended 0.4 px past their headers before (measured at the window's own 125%: 1718.4 against 1718.8 px, now equal). CDP's emulated scale keeps the real scrollbar, so check this through a cleared override.
- A setting row's switch drops its 9 px hover padding on both sides (`.setting-row .mud-switch-span`), so it ends flush with the buttons and selects in the rows around it. A closed select's greyed detail keeps its room with `!important`: MudBlazor's end adornment zeroed the input's right padding, so at 1280 px the monitor name ran under its size. The capture and export panels keep 24 px under their last button (`pt-3 pb-4`).
- Nothing pops in or out. The clip list changes through `letsClip.transition` (the View Transitions API): each card has its own `view-transition-name`, so a saved clip fades in, a deleted one fades out and the rest slide to their places. A delete takes its card off at once, the folder watcher's reload then agrees. Notices and the export's progress and result fade in (`appear`); pages (`.page > *`), the video, the side panels and the timeline strip only fade (`fade-in`), since a moving element adds scrollbars. Every fade and view transition takes 150 ms (`--fade`), the owner's pick over 200 and 250 ms; snackbars fade as quickly and are a little see-through (`MaximumOpacity` 90 in `App`, the owner's pick on 9 November 2026: 92 at first, then 85, which read too see-through). Windows' reduce animations setting turns all of these and the record dot's blink off.
- Saving a cut, Restore Original and the tray balloon opening another clip in the open editor never reload the page, the owner's pick after they flashed. `ClipEditor.LoadAsync` builds everything off screen while `letsClip.detach(true)` holds the current frame on the `player-frozen` canvas (a tainted canvas still draws); the probe, frame times and new strip finish first, then one render inside a view transition swaps it all (`ShowLoaded`), and the canvas fades once the new video has its right frame. `_swapping` keeps `OnAfterRenderAsync` from attaching meanwhile: without it a render during a cut re-attached the old file. Recorded per frame over all three: 0 frames with a spinner, a missing strip or panel, hidden handles or an empty player, the swap taking 300 to 415 ms. Cut and restore make the card's thumbnail before returning (`ReplacedAsync`), so the card is never blank.
- A field says what it holds through its own `Label` on its top edge, never a title above it, the owner's pick after the Start and End fields: every capture select fills the panel's width with a gap of 20 px (`Spacing="5"`), the editor's name reads Name and both device selects read Device. Only rows that pair a title with a short value keep `SettingRow`; the export's one select sits at its row's end as wide as its value (`content-field`), so every export row is a title and its value. A monitor item shows its name centred on the left and its size above its refresh rate on the right.
- Buttons are never outlined: filled for actions, text for quiet ones. Every choice is a `MudSelect`, two items or six, the owner's pick: no button groups (they truncated the row titles at 1280 px), save the Games and Apps switch in its dialog. A select whose items are text gets a `ToStringFunc`, or the field shows the item's markup in the item font. `MudSelect` already opens from `BottomLeft` to `TopLeft`, so no select sets its origins. No ripple.
- Choices that load the PC more read amber, the heaviest red, in the list and the closed field alike (`CapturePanel.ResolutionColor`, `FrameRateColor`, `QualityColor`): above 1080p amber and above 1440p red, 120 and 144 FPS amber and 165 FPS up red, High amber and Max red.
