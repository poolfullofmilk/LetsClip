# LetsClip, Export

Exports, lossless cuts and trim points. Read before touching `ClipExporter`, `ClipLibrary`'s cuts, `SizeBudget`, `TrimRange` or the export panel. Part of `CLAUDE.md`, its rules bind the same way.

## Export

- Fit for Discord is the one choice (`UserSettings.ExportDiscordPlan`, `DiscordPlan`, Off by default), the owner's replacement on 9 November 2026 for Size Limit's 20, 50, 100, 200, 500 MB and Unlimited: Off, Free (20 MB), Nitro Basic (50 MB) or Nitro (500 MB), `SizeBudget.LimitOf`, each with its limit greyed beside it in the list (`ItemLabel`). The closed field is as wide as the picked plan (`content-field`, `field-sizing: content`) and its list grows to fit each plan beside its limit (`RelativeWidth` adaptive), the owner's pick. A plan the selection already fits is disabled in the list, since it would only copy like Off, and the whole select is disabled when no plan changes anything (`ExportPanel.Changes`, `AnyPlanChanges`); a saved plan that changes nothing reads as Off (`Plan`). A file named Off is exactly what was asked for, the owner's words: no loss at all.
- Off, or a selection that already fits its plan (the panel weighs the copy exactly, `SizeBudget.CopyBytes`, see Every Number Checked; the exporter's own first guess is `SizeBudget.KeepsClip`, the selection plus one second of keyframe pre-roll against the limit), is a stream copy (`ClipExporter.CopyAsync` through `ClipLibrary.CopyRangeAsync`, the one recipe the lossless cut shares: `-ss` before `-i`, `-c copy`, the MP4 edit list hiding the pre-roll): the clip's own frames, resolution, frame rate and bitrate. Checked on a 110 MB 1440p60 clip: all 1800 video packets hash identical to the source (`-f framemd5`), and a 15 s selection is a contiguous run of them. The copy's real size is checked against the plan and past it encoding takes over.
- A selection too big for its plan re-encodes to fit at 1080p and 60 FPS at most: `-ss` before `-i` (frame accurate when transcoding), `scale` with Lanczos plus `pad` to even sizes, `yuv420p`, BT.709 tags, AAC 128 kbps, `faststart`. A 30 s, 114 MB 1440p clip came out 18.5 MB for Free and 43.2 MB for Nitro Basic, both 1080p60. Good, Soft or Too Small come from the bit rates below, Too Small refuses with a warning naming the longest selection the plan fits. The Bitrate row's export value is the most an encode gets, video and sound together (`Up to 13,3 Mbps`), or a copy's own; no chip beside it, the owner's pick, so Soft speaks only through the hint under the rows and Too Small through its warning. Muting never raises it: it used to show the video's share alone, which grew when the sound's bits went to the video, and a muted copy leaves out the sound's measured packets (`PacketMap`, from the probe).
- Audio is on or off, the owner's pick (`ExportAudio`, on by default): the row is one filled icon button with no word beside it, grey while on and red while muted like every removal, and the export keeps the sound or leaves it out (`ExportRequest.WithAudio`); there is no export volume. Resolution and Frame Rate are rows of their own, the size the export gets (`2560 × 1440`, the clip's own, or `1920 × 1080` when a plan re-encodes it) and its rate (`60 FPS`), the owner's pick over Output chips.
- Every export row that can differ from the clip is a `ChangedValue`, the owner's pick on 9 November 2026: equal, it reads once in grey; different, the clip's own struck through in red, then the export's in green (`data-changed`; every part is always rendered and CSS hides the strike while equal, so the page script can switch it during a drag). Length (`~~30s~~ 11,4s`), Size (`About ~~110,6 MB~~ 37,5 MB` for a copy, weighed from its packets, see Every Number Checked below; `Up to ~~110,6 MB~~ 20 MB` for an encode), Resolution, Frame Rate and Bitrate use it, and their grey words (`Prefix`: About, Up to) stand left of the struck value and show only with it. Length and a copy's Size follow a drag live (`showValue` and `formatSize` in `AppShell.js`, the size weighed from the packets, `copyBytes`), and Blazor's render on release matches; an encode's Bitrate settles on release. Each part stays whole (`white-space: nowrap`): when a row runs out of room, at the 300 px side panel of a tall window, the new value moves under the struck one, both flush right inside the 40 px row (measured at every sweep size and 1264 x 1150).
- With a selection shorter than the clip the button reads Export and Save, the owner's pick: after a successful export the clip itself is cut to the selection (`ClipLibrary.CutAsync`, Saving the Cut on the progress). The cut is a stream copy with `-ss` before the input (`ClipLibrary.CopyRangeAsync` with `-map 0`, every stream), written to a hidden `.LetsClip-<guid>.mp4` beside the clip (`ClipLibrary.PartialPathIn`, the same name saves and exports use until they are done): FFmpeg 9.0.2 starts at the keyframe before and writes the frames up to the in point with negative times and the discard flag, which the MP4 edit list hides, so it is lossless. It drops any frame that starts before `-ss`, exactly like a re-encode, so both pass the start 1 ms early (`TrimRange.EdgeMargin`). The first cut moves the full clip to `.Originals` in one step with `File.Replace`, so the source always keeps a name; later cuts only replace the cut. A backup belongs to the clip only while `ClipMetadata.ForeignOriginal` is false: metadata re-probed because the visible file changed sets it, so Restore Original is neither offered nor run over an unrelated file, recycling leaves that backup alone, and the next cut sets it aside under `(2)` instead of overwriting it. Every metadata change runs under one lock with its own temporary file (`UpdateMetadata`, `s_metadataGate`) and a cut or restore applies its change to what is stored at that moment, so a rename meanwhile survives. Each cut records where it sits in the original (`ClipMetadata.CutStart`, `CutEnd`, added up over later cuts: 4 to 21.5 s, then 2 to 12 s of that, restores to 6 to 16 s). Restore Original (`Restore`, in the editor header while an original exists) moves it back and puts the trim handles on that cut, the owner's pick, so Save Cut makes it again in one click. Both keep the name, game, date and frame rate, clear the pictures, and reload the editor paused with its volume kept (`LoadAsync(opening: false)`), whose video URL carries the file's write time so WebView2 never plays the old file from cache. Only opening a clip plays it, the owner's pick. The library forgets a clip's metadata only once its folder settles with the file still gone (`ClipLibrary.Settle`), since a cut moves the file away and back.
- Trim points are frame starts. Opening a clip runs one `ffprobe` that reads its streams and every packet's time together (`MediaProbe.ProbeAsync` with `withPackets`, each packet's size and keyframe flag read beside its time, `-show_entries packet=stream_index,pts_time` beside `-show_streams`, no decoding: 0.17 s and 299 KB of JSON for a 30 s 1080p60 clip, the video's 1800 times kept from 3208 packets and sorted, a time that reads N/A skipped), where it used to run a second ffprobe for the times alone, and the page script snaps Start and End to the nearest one within half a step, else keeps the time (a still window can hold one frame for seconds). Set Start Here takes the frame on screen, Set End Here the start of the frame after it, so the frame you see is the last one kept; the player shows a start half a millisecond in and an end half a millisecond before it, since a seek to an exact frame time showed the frame before in two of six tries. A clip whose sound runs on less than a step past its picture is as long as its picture (`MediaProbe`), so a cut reads the length its selection did: a 5,2s selection used to come back 5,3s, its sound 18 ms longer. Checked with a clip whose every frame carries its own number in its brightness: cuts and exports kept exactly the frames from Start up to End for every range tried, through the Set buttons, typed times and mouse drags alike. `-shortest` on the cut dropped the last frame whenever the sound ended first.
- The frame rate select went with Size Limit (`ExportFullFrameRate`, the Ponytail audit of 9 November 2026): a copy keeps the clip's own rate, and an encode for a Discord limit stops at 60, where the bits do most.
- `SizeBudget` turns the limit (decimal MB) into a video bitrate after a 5% margin, the MP4 index and audio. Bits per pixel decide Good, Soft or Too Small (Sharp went in that audit, nothing ever told it from Good); Too Small refuses.
- The hardware encoder goes first (single pass VBR aimed at 90% of the budget, since AMF overshoots), x264 two pass second. A pass whose progress stops for 15 s is killed and counts as a failed encode (`ClipExporter.RunPassAsync`), so a hung AMF export moves on to x264 instead of waiting out the 30 minute limit; one hung that way in the November 2026 sweep. Every result is probed and its real size checked; over the limit retries at a lower bitrate, twice per encoder. An export is named by its clip, never by its limit (`LetsClip 5-11-2026 at 21h10`, then `(2)`). Cancelling mid-encode once reported "Export failed, the file could not be written" and left the partial file: the killed encoder's file stays locked a moment, so `FFmpeg.DeleteOutputAsync` retries for two seconds and never replaces the real outcome. On a 30 second noise heavy 1080p60 test AMF landed 18.4 MB for 20 MB and 9.3 MB for 10 MB on the first try in about 9 s, and scored VMAF 85 against x264 two pass 74 at the same size (28 s). A 12 s 1080p120 test pattern landed 8.7 MB for 10 MB at 60 FPS.

## Every Number Checked Against The Files, 12 November 2026

The owner asked whether the numbers are always right. A throwaway driver set the editor through a matrix on six clips (the 1440p60 and noisy 1080p60 test clips, the 720p30 and portrait x264 test clips, and real 5 and 10 minute recordings): the whole clip and a 13,5 s, 2m15s, 0,9 s and 18 s selection, Off, Free, Nitro Basic and Nitro, sound on and off, with real presses on the plan select and the Audio button. It read every row, exported, and compared each export with FFprobe. Where a line above says otherwise, this section wins.

- Right every time: Length to the frame on LetsClip's own recordings and the `-bf 0` clips (30.000, 13.500, 300.000, 600.000 s), Resolution and Frame Rate, sound present or not, every plan export under its limit (17.5 to 18.8 MB for Free, 44.0 MB for Nitro Basic) and under its `Up to` bitrate.
- Wrong, and fixed: a copy's Size and Bitrate were the clip's average rate times the selection, so a quiet or busy stretch of a real recording was off: a 2m15s cut read About 30,2 MB and came out 36,1 MB, an 18 s busy stretch read 4,5 MB and came out 10 MB. A copy is now weighed exactly: the editor's one probe reads every packet's size and keyframe flag beside its time (`-show_entries packet=stream_index,pts_time,size,flags`, `PacketMap`), and `SizeBudget.CopyBytes` adds the video packets FFmpeg's copy really takes (from the keyframe at or before the seek point to the stop), 12 bytes of MP4 index per packet, the sound's measured bytes and packets per second from that same keyframe on (see On The Gaming PC below), and 1000 bytes of headers. The page script weighs a drag the same way from running totals (`copyBytes` in `AppShell.js`); live and rendered values matched on six ranges of the 10 minute clip. Measured against every copy export: within 0.00 to 0.06% on LetsClip's own recordings and every whole-clip copy, the 18 s stretch 9.999 against 9.997 MB. The copy's Bitrate row is its weight over its length, and whether a plan changes anything is that same weight against the limit, so a plan never re-encodes a selection that a copy would fit.
- Wrong on clips not made by LetsClip, by nature: x264's dense scene change I-frames read as keyframes in FFprobe but are not the seek points FFmpeg's copy uses, so 0,9 s copies of the noisy test clips came out 8% over the weight, and the x264 `veryslow` test clips (8 B frames) up to 20% on files under 100 KB. LetsClip's own recordings (an IDR every second, no B frames) are exact.
- A copy of a clip with B frames keeps up to 7 frames past End, not two: the `veryslow` test clips' 54 s cut came out 54.234 s. LetsClip's own cuts stay frame exact.
- `Up to 1,1 Mbps` stood over a 1.12 Mbps file whose real ceiling was 1.125: ceilings are now rounded up (`Up to 1,2 Mbps`).
- Free on a 5 or 10 minute selection is Too Small (about 0.5 Mbps of picture at 1080p60), and Export stayed disabled behind a small red caption the owner missed. It is now a warning `Notice` that names the longest selection the plan fits (`SizeBudget.LongestSeconds`, the budget solved for length at the softest picture it allows, 2m16s for Free on a 10 minute 1080p 59 FPS recording with sound, checked by a self test one step either side).
- A muted copy's weight leaves out the sound's measured packets and their index entries, where it used to subtract the stream's nominal bit rate.

## On The Gaming PC, 12 November 2026

The same check on real AMF recordings (7800X3D, RX 9070 XT, a 5 minute 1080p60 monitor clip of 99.2 MB with system audio), exports compared with FFprobe and with a throwaway that weighs like `SizeBudget.CopyBytes` and makes the real copy beside it.

- Wrong, and fixed: a short copy with sound read under its file, 267 KB shown for a 0,9 s selection that came out 275 KB (2.8% under), 5.6% under on a 1 s one. FFmpeg's seek lands every stream on the video keyframe before the cut and a copy keeps all of it, so the sound's lead in is in the file too, hidden by the edit list like the picture's. `SizeBudget.CopyBytes` and `copyBytes` in `AppShell.js` now weigh the sound over the selection plus that lead (`seek` less the keyframe's time), with a self test. After it: 0,9 s reads 274 KB against 274,705 bytes, 2m15s 40,6 MB against 40,579,695, nine ranges within 0.03 to 0.31%, and four drags read the same live as rendered. The laptop's ranges happened to start near a keyframe.
- A muted copy reads 0.15 to 0.31% over (its index and headers are smaller than the 12 bytes a packet and 1000 bytes measured with sound): 3,3 MB shown for 3,241,378 bytes. Left, it is under one shown digit.
- Nitro Basic on the whole 5 minute clip: AMF landed 46.3 MB for 50 MB on its first try in 90.2 s (about 200 FPS) with capture running, 18,000 frames, 300.000 s. Quick Sync's overshoot does not happen on AMF. The first run of the same export stopped responding twice after its first progress line (15.6 s each, the stall guard) and x264 two pass finished it at 47.8 MB in 149 s; the same command by hand and two more runs in the app then ran clean, so it is the known AMF hang, not the length.
- Free on the 10 minute clip reads `Too long for Free, which fits about 2m14s, pick a shorter selection` with Export off, the Notice three balanced lines in the 300 px panel.
- An Off copy of a 2m15s selection took 0.2 s to write, 2.6 s with its cut and reload.

## Best Encoder And Size, Measured 12 And 13 November 2026

The owner asked for the best possible export, and the most consistent one. A throwaway Node driver encoded two real Overwatch clips (a 12 s fight at 50 Mbps and 30 s of a 60 s clip at 28 Mbps, both 1080p60) with the export's own arguments and scored each against its source with `libvmaf` at 1080p (6 points is about one visible step). - The encoder's settings were already the best H.264 gives: VBAQ changed nothing to the byte, the `high_quality` preset lost 0.5, full pre-analysis (`pa_lookahead_buffer_depth 40`, TAQ, CAQ) gained 3 at 12 Mbps and lost 3 at 2.5, x264 `slow` and `slower` gained 1 to 2 over `medium` for two to five times the wait, and `hqvbr` ignores the bitrate (5.3 Mbps whatever was asked). Left as they were.
- The size is what decides it, so an encode now picks its lines from its bits (`SizeBudget.Plan`, `ExportBudget.Lines`): 1080p from 0.06 bits per 1080p pixel and frame (7.5 Mbps at 60 FPS), 720p below that, 540p below 0.012 (1.5 Mbps), never above the clip's own size (`ClipExporter.OutputSize`). 720p scored 6 to 8 higher at 2.5 Mbps and 2 to 6 at 5, the same at 8 and 2 lower at 12; 540p matches 720p at 2.5 and is the only size AMF writes at the asked size below 1.5 Mbps, since AMF H.264 has a floor (about 1.7 to 2 Mbps at 1080p, 1.2 at 720p, 0.7 at 540p), which at 1080p sent a long clip through two oversized tries to x264 at a score of 8 to 16. The budget, Good, Soft and Too Long still read the 1080p shape, so no limit moved. The Resolution row shows the size picked (`1920 × 1080` struck, `1280 × 720`).
- AMF stays first and x264 two pass second: AMF wins below 8 Mbps at every size, x264 by 1 to 4 above it at three to five times the wait. AMF is repeatable to the byte (12 runs each of two encodes, the same size and score every time).
- HEVC and AV1 on AMF score 9 to 16 higher at every size. Not used: Discord plays H.264 everywhere, HEVC and AV1 only where the viewer's device decodes them, and the owner asked for consistent. The owner's pick if that ever changes.
- In the app, sandbox, AMF on the first try each time: 60 s for Free 18.7 MB at 1280 × 720 in 14.5 s, the 12 s fight for Free 18 MB at 1920 × 1080 in 4.8 s, 5 minutes for Nitro Basic 47.3 MB at 960 × 540 in 66 s (90 s at 1080p before), 60 s for Nitro Basic 46.5 MB at 720p in 13 s.
- Size and Bitrate of an encode read `About`, like a copy, the owner's ask (`Up to` over a file that came out 6 to 14% smaller read as wrong): the size is where encodes land, 92% of the limit (`ExportBudget.TypicalBytes`, measured 86 to 95% over eleven exports), and the bitrate is that size over the length, so both rows always agree (`About 18,4 MB` and `About 2,5 Mbps` over an 18.7 MB, 2.50 Mbps file). `Formatter.FormatBitrate`'s `roundUp` went with the ceiling.
- A retry leads the progress line (`Try 2, Encoding on AMD AMF`), the owner's ask: behind the phase it was cut off.
- An AMF encode hung twice during these tests, both while the owner played and another AMF session ran; the second only because the first hung process was still alive. The stall guard and the x264 fallback cover it in the app. The hung test process kept the owner's clip open for two hours, so his Save Cut said the clip may be in use: tests read copies of clips, never the owner's own files (`Docs/Verify.md`).
