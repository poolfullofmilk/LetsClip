# LetsClip, Export

Exports, lossless cuts and trim points. Read before touching `ClipExporter`, `ClipLibrary`'s cuts, `SizeBudget`, `TrimRange` or the export panel. Part of `CLAUDE.md`, its rules bind the same way.

## Export

- Fit for Discord is the one choice (`UserSettings.ExportDiscordPlan`, `DiscordPlan`, Off by default), the owner's replacement on 9 November 2026 for Size Limit's 20, 50, 100, 200, 500 MB and Unlimited: Off, Free (20 MB), Nitro Basic (50 MB) or Nitro (1 GB, 500 MB until 14 November 2026, when the owner's own Nitro trial read 1 GB), `SizeBudget.LimitOf`, each with its limit greyed beside it in the list (`ItemLabel`). The closed field is as wide as the picked plan (`content-field`, `field-sizing: content`) and its list grows to fit each plan beside its limit (`RelativeWidth` adaptive), the owner's pick. A plan the selection already fits is disabled in the list, since it would only copy like Off, and the whole select is disabled when no plan changes anything (`ExportPanel.Changes`, `AnyPlanChanges`); a saved plan that changes nothing reads as Off (`Plan`). A file named Off is exactly what was asked for, the owner's words: no loss at all.
- Off, or a selection that already fits its plan (the panel weighs the copy exactly, `SizeBudget.CopyBytes`, see `Docs/History.md`, Export: Every Number Checked Against The Files; the exporter's own first guess is `SizeBudget.KeepsClip`, the selection plus one second of keyframe pre-roll against the limit), is a stream copy (`ClipExporter.CopyAsync` through `ClipLibrary.CopyRangeAsync`, the one recipe the lossless cut shares: `-ss` before `-i`, `-c copy`, the MP4 edit list hiding the pre-roll): the clip's own frames, resolution, frame rate and bitrate. Checked on a 110 MB 1440p60 clip: all 1800 video packets hash identical to the source (`-f framemd5`), and a 15 s selection is a contiguous run of them. The copy's real size is checked against the plan and past it encoding takes over.
- A selection too big for its plan re-encodes to fit as HEVC (see `Docs/History.md`, Export: Background Exports, Faster AMF, HEVC And AV1 Checked), at the lines and frame rate its bits allow (1080p, 720p, 540p, 360p and 240p at 60 FPS, then 240p at 30 and 15, `SizeBudget.Plan`, see `Docs/History.md`, Export: Every Selection Fits And The Video Switch): `-ss` before `-i` (frame accurate when transcoding), `scale` with Lanczos plus `pad` to even sizes, `yuv420p`, BT.709 tags, AAC at 128 kbps or less, `faststart`. Good, Soft or Too Small come from the bit rates below, Too Small refuses with a warning naming the longest selection the plan fits. The Bitrate row's export value is where an encode lands over its length (`About 2,5 Mbps`, see `Docs/History.md`, Export: Best Encoder And Size, Measured 12 And 13 November); for a copy it is the clip's own bitrate, less any sound track the export leaves out (`ExportPanel.CopyBitsPerSecond`), the owner's word of 14 November 2026 (`whatever makes the most sense`), so Off never reads a different bitrate for the same frames; the clip's own bitrate is what plays since 22 November 2026, its frames from zero on and its measured sound, never the hidden keyframe pre-roll (`Docs/Behaviour.md`). For the sound alone under a plan it is the Opus rate, the plan's sound bitrate or the source's own when that is less, and its Size the Opus target or the source's sound when that is less (`SizeBudget.Plan` gives sound alone a target of its own bits, never the whole limit). No chip beside it, the owner's pick, so Soft speaks only through the hint under the rows and Too Small through its warning.
- Audio is on or off, the owner's pick (`ExportAudio`, on by default): the row is one filled icon button with no word beside it, grey while on and red while muted like every removal; a single track has no export volume, since every re-encoded sound is evened to -14 LUFS anyway. A clip with three sound tracks shows two volume buttons in that row instead, the game and the microphone (`ExportMicrophone`, on by default, `Docs/History.md`, Capture: Three Sound Tracks): a press mutes or unmutes, sliding to zero mutes and keeps the last level, and while both are kept their levels balance them (`ExportGameVolume`, `ExportMicrophoneVolume`, 10% to 100% in tenths, remembered), since 20 November 2026. Both at 100% keep the recorded mix track (`ExportRequest.AudioTrack` 0); one alone keeps its own track as recorded; both kept at other levels rebuild the mix from the game and microphone tracks at those levels (`ExportRequest.IsBalanced`, `ClipExporter.SoundGraph`, `amix` with `normalize=0` and the peak limiter), its loudness then evened like any encode. Under Off the picture still copies exactly and only the sound encodes (AAC at 128 kbps, `ClipLibrary.CopyRangeAsync` with a sound codec after `-c copy`), so the main button reads Export rather than Copy File. Measured on 20 November 2026: the game at 50% lowered its 330 Hz tone 6.0 dB against the microphone's 660 Hz one, the H.264 picture copied at its own 16 Mbps. Muting both leaves the sound out. A Video row above them leaves out the picture since 17 November 2026 (see `Docs/History.md`, Export: Every Selection Fits And The Video Switch). Resolution and Frame Rate are rows of their own, the size the export gets (`2560 × 1440`, the clip's own, `2550 × 1434` for a crop under Off, or `1920 × 1080` when a plan re-encodes it) and its rate (`60 FPS`), the owner's pick over Output chips.
- Every export row that can differ from the clip is a `ChangedValue`, the owner's pick on 9 November 2026: equal, it reads once in grey; different, the clip's own struck through in red, then the export's in green (`data-changed`; every part is always rendered and CSS hides the strike while equal, so the page script can switch it during a drag). Length (`~~30s~~ 11,4s`), Size (`About ~~110,6 MB~~ 37,5 MB` for a copy, weighed from its packets, see `Docs/History.md`, Export: Every Number Checked Against The Files; `About ~~110,6 MB~~ 18,4 MB` for an encode, where encodes land), Resolution, Frame Rate and Bitrate use it, and their grey words (`Prefix`: About, Up to) stand left of the struck value and show only with it. Every row follows a trim or crop drag as it happens, the owner's words of 21 November 2026 (the numbers on the right live, `not after some time or click`): the page script tells Blazor the shown range or crop one call at a time, the newest waiting (`OnRanging`, `OnCropping`), and only the Details panel redraws from them (`ExportPanel.ShowLive`, the whole editor before 22 November 2026), the handles and the box staying the script's; until then only Length and a copy's Size followed, written by the page script, and an encode's numbers settled on release. Each part stays whole (`white-space: nowrap`): when a row runs out of room, at the 300 px side panel of a tall window, the new value moves under the struck one, both flush right inside the 40 px row (measured at every sweep size and 1264 x 1150).
- With a selection shorter than the clip the button reads Export and Save, the owner's pick: after a successful export the clip itself is cut to the selection (`ClipLibrary.CutAsync`, Saving the Cut on the progress). The cut is a stream copy with `-ss` before the input (`ClipLibrary.CopyRangeAsync` with `-map 0`, every stream), written to a hidden `.LetsClip-<guid>.mp4` beside the clip (`ClipLibrary.PartialPathIn`, the same name saves and exports use until they are done): FFmpeg 9.0.2 starts at the keyframe before and writes the frames up to the in point with negative times and the discard flag, which the MP4 edit list hides, so it is lossless. It drops any frame that starts before `-ss`, exactly like a re-encode, so both pass the start 1 ms early (`TrimRange.EdgeMargin`). The first cut moves the full clip to `.Originals` in one step with `File.Replace`, so the source always keeps a name; later cuts only replace the cut. A backup belongs to the clip only while `ClipMetadata.ForeignOriginal` is false: metadata re-probed because the visible file changed sets it, so Restore Original is neither offered nor run over an unrelated file, recycling leaves that backup alone, and the next cut sets it aside under `(2)` instead of overwriting it. Every metadata change runs under one lock with its own temporary file (`UpdateMetadata`, `s_metadataGate`) and a cut or restore applies its change to what is stored at that moment, so a rename meanwhile survives. Each cut records where it sits in the original (`ClipMetadata.CutStart`, `CutEnd`, added up over later cuts: 4 to 21.5 s, then 2 to 12 s of that, restores to 6 to 16 s). Restore Original (`Restore`, in the editor header while an original exists) moves it back and puts the trim handles on that cut, the owner's pick, so Save Cut makes it again in one click. Both keep the name, game, date and frame rate, clear the pictures, and reload the editor paused with its volume kept (`LoadAsync(opening: false)`), whose video URL carries the file's write time so WebView2 never plays the old file from cache. Only opening a clip plays it, the owner's pick. The library forgets a clip's metadata only once its folder settles with the file still gone (`ClipLibrary.Settle`), since a cut moves the file away and back.
- Trim points are frame starts. Opening a clip ran one `ffprobe` that reads its streams and every packet's time together until 16 November 2026, when the packets moved to a run of their own (see `Docs/History.md`, The Packet Listing) (`MediaProbe.ProbeAsync` with `withPackets`, each packet's size and keyframe flag read beside its time, `-show_entries packet=stream_index,pts_time` beside `-show_streams`, no decoding: 0.17 s and 299 KB of JSON for a 30 s 1080p60 clip, the video's 1800 times kept from 3208 packets and sorted, a time that reads N/A skipped), where it used to run a second ffprobe for the times alone, and the page script snaps Start and End to the nearest one within half a step, else keeps the time (a still window can hold one frame for seconds). Set Start Here takes the frame on screen, Set End Here the start of the frame after it, so the frame you see is the last one kept; the player shows a start half a millisecond in and an end half a millisecond before it, since a seek to an exact frame time showed the frame before in two of six tries. A clip whose sound runs on less than a step past its picture is as long as its picture (`MediaProbe`), so a cut reads the length its selection did: a 5,2s selection used to come back 5,3s, its sound 18 ms longer. Checked with a clip whose every frame carries its own number in its brightness: cuts and exports kept exactly the frames from Start up to End for every range tried, through the Set buttons, typed times and mouse drags alike. `-shortest` on the cut dropped the last frame whenever the sound ended first.
- The frame rate select went with Size Limit (`ExportFullFrameRate`, the Ponytail audit of 9 November 2026): a copy and a crop under Off keep the clip's own rate, and an encode for a Discord limit stops at 60, where the bits do most.
- `SizeBudget` turns the limit (decimal MB) into a video and a sound bitrate after a 5% margin and the MP4 index. Bits per pixel decide Good or Soft (Sharp went in that audit, nothing ever told it from Good); Too Small is only a selection under the lowest rung, and refuses.
- The card's HEVC goes first, then its H.264 (single pass VBR aimed at 90% of the budget, since AMF overshoots), x264 two pass last. A pass whose progress stops for 15 s is killed and counts as a failed encode (`ClipExporter.RunPassAsync`), so a hung AMF export moves on to x264 instead of waiting out the 30 minute limit; one hung that way in the November 2026 sweep. Every result is probed and its real size checked; over the limit retries at a lower bitrate, twice per encoder. An export is named like its clip's file, never by its limit (`Overwatch 2026-11-14 21-05-33.mp4` in `Exports`, then `(2)`), since 14 November 2026 when clip files took their names (`LetsClip 5-11-2026 at 21h10` before). Cancelling mid-encode once reported "Export failed, the file could not be written" and left the partial file: the killed encoder's file stays locked a moment, so `FFmpeg.DeleteOutputAsync` retries for two seconds and never replaces the real outcome. On a 30 second noise heavy 1080p60 test AMF landed 18.4 MB for 20 MB and 9.3 MB for 10 MB on the first try in about 9 s, and scored VMAF 85 against x264 two pass 74 at the same size (28 s). A 12 s 1080p120 test pattern landed 8.7 MB for 10 MB at 60 FPS.
- Export Time, the last export row, says how long the export will take (`About 7s`): an encode from its pixels over the last encode's measured pace (`ClipExporter.EncodeSeconds`, `PixelsOf`), each output frame its own pixels and each source frame half a megapixel more for decoding it (`FramePixels`, so a tiny output still costs its source frames), the pace timed over the encode alone (`s_pixelsPerSecond`), plus a second of probing, testing and checking, plus the sound's loudness pass at 700 times real time and, for the sound alone, Opus at 200; a copy from its size at 400 MB/s (`CopySeconds`), rounded to whole seconds under 10, to fives under a minute and to tens past it. The first export of a run takes a usual pace, 500 megapixels a second with a graphics card encoder and 160 with x264 alone; on the 7800X3D with AMF the paces measured 675 to 765 since 22 November 2026, from a 250 x 250 crop of a 1080p60 minute (3.5 s, read About 4s) to 30 minutes of 240 x 140 (77 s, read About 1m30s, where the old pixels only model read About 10s and then, taught by it, read a 1080p minute About 2m30s that took 13 s). Too Long reads `-`, and while the button only copies (Copy File, the export being the clip itself) it reads None, since nothing is exported (it read About 1s until 22 November 2026).
- The crop box is the export's crop: always over the picture, sized from its corners and sides, moved by a drag inside, undone by dragging it back over the whole picture, with no Reset Crop since 22 November 2026 (`Docs/Layout.md`, `Docs/Behaviour.md`); a crop always encodes and its numbers follow every drag. Under Off it keeps its own pixels and the clip's frame rate (`SizeBudget.Keep`), only a plan fits it into the 1080p box at 60 FPS; a card's Copy for Discord whose crop fits its plan, its floor included, exports it like Off, as the editor does (`ClipExporter.ForDiscord`). Off weighs the crop from the selection's own frames times the share of the picture kept, plus one 128 kbps track while sound is kept (`ClipExporter.CropBytes`, `SizeBudget.PictureBytes`), so muting changes its Size by the sound alone, and a selection reads the same before and after it is cut (16,6 MB for 10 to 41,4 s of the owner's Discord clip either way; it read 15,3 MB before the cut and 17 MB after, weighed as a share of the whole file). The editor hands that weight to the exporter (`ExportRequest.CropBytes`), which probes without packets, so both aim at the same number, and Off keeps full 128 kbps sound beside it (`SizeBudget.Keep`; a long crop's sound fell to 57 kbps before).
