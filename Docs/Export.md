# LetsClip, Export

Exports, lossless cuts and trim points. Read before touching `ClipExporter`, `ClipLibrary`'s cuts, `SizeBudget`, `TrimRange` or the export panel. Part of `CLAUDE.md`, its rules bind the same way.

## Export

- Fit for Discord is the one choice (`UserSettings.ExportDiscordPlan`, `DiscordPlan`, Off by default), the owner's replacement on 9 November 2026 for Size Limit's 20, 50, 100, 200, 500 MB and Unlimited: Off, Free (20 MB), Nitro Basic (50 MB) or Nitro (1 GB, 500 MB until 14 November 2026, when the owner's own Nitro trial read 1 GB), `SizeBudget.LimitOf`, each with its limit greyed beside it in the list (`ItemLabel`). The closed field is as wide as the picked plan (`content-field`, `field-sizing: content`) and its list grows to fit each plan beside its limit (`RelativeWidth` adaptive), the owner's pick. A plan the selection already fits is disabled in the list, since it would only copy like Off, and the whole select is disabled when no plan changes anything (`ExportPanel.Changes`, `AnyPlanChanges`); a saved plan that changes nothing reads as Off (`Plan`). A file named Off is exactly what was asked for, the owner's words: no loss at all.
- Off, or a selection that already fits its plan (the panel weighs the copy exactly, `SizeBudget.CopyBytes`, see Every Number Checked; the exporter's own first guess is `SizeBudget.KeepsClip`, the selection plus one second of keyframe pre-roll against the limit), is a stream copy (`ClipExporter.CopyAsync` through `ClipLibrary.CopyRangeAsync`, the one recipe the lossless cut shares: `-ss` before `-i`, `-c copy`, the MP4 edit list hiding the pre-roll): the clip's own frames, resolution, frame rate and bitrate. Checked on a 110 MB 1440p60 clip: all 1800 video packets hash identical to the source (`-f framemd5`), and a 15 s selection is a contiguous run of them. The copy's real size is checked against the plan and past it encoding takes over.
- A selection too big for its plan re-encodes to fit as HEVC (see Background Exports), at the lines and frame rate its bits allow (1080p, 720p, 540p, 360p and 240p at 60 FPS, then 240p at 30 and 15, `SizeBudget.Plan`, see Every Selection Fits, 17 November 2026): `-ss` before `-i` (frame accurate when transcoding), `scale` with Lanczos plus `pad` to even sizes, `yuv420p`, BT.709 tags, AAC at 128 kbps or less, `faststart`. Good, Soft or Too Small come from the bit rates below, Too Small refuses with a warning naming the longest selection the plan fits. The Bitrate row's export value is where an encode lands over its length (`About 2,5 Mbps`, see Best Encoder And Size); for a copy it is the clip's own bitrate, less any sound track the export leaves out (`ExportPanel.CopyBitsPerSecond`), the owner's word of 14 November 2026 (`whatever makes the most sense`), so Off never reads a different bitrate for the same frames. No chip beside it, the owner's pick, so Soft speaks only through the hint under the rows and Too Small through its warning.
- Audio is on or off, the owner's pick (`ExportAudio`, on by default): the row is one filled icon button with no word beside it, grey while on and red while muted like every removal; there is no export volume. A clip with three sound tracks shows Game Audio and Microphone rows instead (`ExportMicrophone`, on by default, `Docs/Capture.md`, Three Sound Tracks), and the export keeps the one track they pick (`ExportRequest.AudioTrack`); muting both leaves the sound out. Resolution and Frame Rate are rows of their own, the size the export gets (`2560 × 1440`, the clip's own, or `1920 × 1080` when a plan re-encodes it) and its rate (`60 FPS`), the owner's pick over Output chips.
- Every export row that can differ from the clip is a `ChangedValue`, the owner's pick on 9 November 2026: equal, it reads once in grey; different, the clip's own struck through in red, then the export's in green (`data-changed`; every part is always rendered and CSS hides the strike while equal, so the page script can switch it during a drag). Length (`~~30s~~ 11,4s`), Size (`About ~~110,6 MB~~ 37,5 MB` for a copy, weighed from its packets, see Every Number Checked below; `About ~~110,6 MB~~ 18,4 MB` for an encode, where encodes land), Resolution, Frame Rate and Bitrate use it, and their grey words (`Prefix`: About, Up to) stand left of the struck value and show only with it. Length and a copy's Size follow a drag live (`showValue` and `formatSize` in `AppShell.js`, the size weighed from the packets, `copyBytes`), and Blazor's render on release matches; an encode's Bitrate settles on release. Each part stays whole (`white-space: nowrap`): when a row runs out of room, at the 300 px side panel of a tall window, the new value moves under the struck one, both flush right inside the 40 px row (measured at every sweep size and 1264 x 1150).
- With a selection shorter than the clip the button reads Export and Save, the owner's pick: after a successful export the clip itself is cut to the selection (`ClipLibrary.CutAsync`, Saving the Cut on the progress). The cut is a stream copy with `-ss` before the input (`ClipLibrary.CopyRangeAsync` with `-map 0`, every stream), written to a hidden `.LetsClip-<guid>.mp4` beside the clip (`ClipLibrary.PartialPathIn`, the same name saves and exports use until they are done): FFmpeg 9.0.2 starts at the keyframe before and writes the frames up to the in point with negative times and the discard flag, which the MP4 edit list hides, so it is lossless. It drops any frame that starts before `-ss`, exactly like a re-encode, so both pass the start 1 ms early (`TrimRange.EdgeMargin`). The first cut moves the full clip to `.Originals` in one step with `File.Replace`, so the source always keeps a name; later cuts only replace the cut. A backup belongs to the clip only while `ClipMetadata.ForeignOriginal` is false: metadata re-probed because the visible file changed sets it, so Restore Original is neither offered nor run over an unrelated file, recycling leaves that backup alone, and the next cut sets it aside under `(2)` instead of overwriting it. Every metadata change runs under one lock with its own temporary file (`UpdateMetadata`, `s_metadataGate`) and a cut or restore applies its change to what is stored at that moment, so a rename meanwhile survives. Each cut records where it sits in the original (`ClipMetadata.CutStart`, `CutEnd`, added up over later cuts: 4 to 21.5 s, then 2 to 12 s of that, restores to 6 to 16 s). Restore Original (`Restore`, in the editor header while an original exists) moves it back and puts the trim handles on that cut, the owner's pick, so Save Cut makes it again in one click. Both keep the name, game, date and frame rate, clear the pictures, and reload the editor paused with its volume kept (`LoadAsync(opening: false)`), whose video URL carries the file's write time so WebView2 never plays the old file from cache. Only opening a clip plays it, the owner's pick. The library forgets a clip's metadata only once its folder settles with the file still gone (`ClipLibrary.Settle`), since a cut moves the file away and back.
- Trim points are frame starts. Opening a clip ran one `ffprobe` that reads its streams and every packet's time together until 16 November 2026, when the packets moved to a run of their own (see The Packet Listing) (`MediaProbe.ProbeAsync` with `withPackets`, each packet's size and keyframe flag read beside its time, `-show_entries packet=stream_index,pts_time` beside `-show_streams`, no decoding: 0.17 s and 299 KB of JSON for a 30 s 1080p60 clip, the video's 1800 times kept from 3208 packets and sorted, a time that reads N/A skipped), where it used to run a second ffprobe for the times alone, and the page script snaps Start and End to the nearest one within half a step, else keeps the time (a still window can hold one frame for seconds). Set Start Here takes the frame on screen, Set End Here the start of the frame after it, so the frame you see is the last one kept; the player shows a start half a millisecond in and an end half a millisecond before it, since a seek to an exact frame time showed the frame before in two of six tries. A clip whose sound runs on less than a step past its picture is as long as its picture (`MediaProbe`), so a cut reads the length its selection did: a 5,2s selection used to come back 5,3s, its sound 18 ms longer. Checked with a clip whose every frame carries its own number in its brightness: cuts and exports kept exactly the frames from Start up to End for every range tried, through the Set buttons, typed times and mouse drags alike. `-shortest` on the cut dropped the last frame whenever the sound ended first.
- The frame rate select went with Size Limit (`ExportFullFrameRate`, the Ponytail audit of 9 November 2026): a copy keeps the clip's own rate, and an encode for a Discord limit stops at 60, where the bits do most.
- `SizeBudget` turns the limit (decimal MB) into a video and a sound bitrate after a 5% margin and the MP4 index. Bits per pixel decide Good or Soft (Sharp went in that audit, nothing ever told it from Good); Too Small is only a selection under the lowest rung, and refuses.
- The card's HEVC goes first, then its H.264 (single pass VBR aimed at 90% of the budget, since AMF overshoots), x264 two pass last. A pass whose progress stops for 15 s is killed and counts as a failed encode (`ClipExporter.RunPassAsync`), so a hung AMF export moves on to x264 instead of waiting out the 30 minute limit; one hung that way in the November 2026 sweep. Every result is probed and its real size checked; over the limit retries at a lower bitrate, twice per encoder. An export is named like its clip's file, never by its limit (`Overwatch 2026-11-14 21-05-33.mp4` in `Exports`, then `(2)`), since 14 November 2026 when clip files took their names (`LetsClip 5-11-2026 at 21h10` before). Cancelling mid-encode once reported "Export failed, the file could not be written" and left the partial file: the killed encoder's file stays locked a moment, so `FFmpeg.DeleteOutputAsync` retries for two seconds and never replaces the real outcome. On a 30 second noise heavy 1080p60 test AMF landed 18.4 MB for 20 MB and 9.3 MB for 10 MB on the first try in about 9 s, and scored VMAF 85 against x264 two pass 74 at the same size (28 s). A 12 s 1080p120 test pattern landed 8.7 MB for 10 MB at 60 FPS.

## Every Number Checked Against The Files, 12 November 2026

The owner asked whether the numbers are always right. A throwaway driver set the editor through a matrix on six clips (the 1440p60 and noisy 1080p60 test clips, the 720p30 and portrait x264 test clips, and real 5 and 10 minute recordings): the whole clip and a 13,5 s, 2m15s, 0,9 s and 18 s selection, Off, Free, Nitro Basic and Nitro, sound on and off, with real presses on the plan select and the Audio button. It read every row, exported, and compared each export with FFprobe.

- Right every time: Length to the frame on LetsClip's own recordings and the `-bf 0` clips (30.000, 13.500, 300.000, 600.000 s), Resolution and Frame Rate, sound present or not, every plan export under its limit (17.5 to 18.8 MB for Free, 44.0 MB for Nitro Basic) and under its `Up to` bitrate.
- Wrong, and fixed: a copy's Size and Bitrate were the clip's average rate times the selection, so a quiet or busy stretch of a real recording was off: a 2m15s cut read About 30,2 MB and came out 36,1 MB, an 18 s busy stretch read 4,5 MB and came out 10 MB. A copy is now weighed exactly: the editor's one probe reads every packet's size and keyframe flag beside its time (`-show_entries packet=stream_index,pts_time,size,flags`, `PacketMap`), and `SizeBudget.CopyBytes` adds the video packets FFmpeg's copy really takes (from the keyframe at or before the seek point to the stop), 12 bytes of MP4 index per packet, the sound's measured bytes and packets per second from that same keyframe on, times the sound tracks the copy keeps (see On The Gaming PC below), and 1000 bytes of headers. The page script weighs a drag the same way from running totals (`copyBytes` in `AppShell.js`); live and rendered values matched on six ranges of the 10 minute clip. Measured against every copy export: within 0.00 to 0.06% on LetsClip's own recordings and every whole-clip copy, the 18 s stretch 9.999 against 9.997 MB. The copy's Bitrate row was its weight over its length until 14 November 2026 (now the clip's own, see Export above), and whether a plan changes anything is that same weight against the limit, so a plan never re-encodes a selection that a copy would fit.
- Wrong on clips not made by LetsClip, by nature: x264's dense scene change I-frames read as keyframes in FFprobe but are not the seek points FFmpeg's copy uses, so 0,9 s copies of the noisy test clips came out 8% over the weight, and the x264 `veryslow` test clips (8 B frames) up to 20% on files under 100 KB. LetsClip's own recordings (an IDR every second, no B frames) are exact.
- A copy of a clip with B frames keeps up to 7 frames past End, not two: the `veryslow` test clips' 54 s cut came out 54.234 s. LetsClip's own cuts stay frame exact.
- `Up to 1,1 Mbps` stood over a 1.12 Mbps file whose real ceiling was 1.125: ceilings are now rounded up (`Up to 1,2 Mbps`).
- Free on a 5 or 10 minute selection is Too Small (about 0.5 Mbps of picture at 1080p60), and Export stayed disabled behind a small red caption the owner missed. It is now a warning `Notice` that names the longest selection the plan fits (`SizeBudget.LongestSeconds`, the budget solved for length at the softest picture it allows, 2m16s for Free on a 10 minute 1080p 59 FPS recording with sound, checked by a self test one step either side).
- A muted copy's weight leaves out the sound's measured packets and their index entries, where it used to subtract the stream's nominal bit rate.

The same check on real AMF recordings (7800X3D, RX 9070 XT, a 5 minute 1080p60 monitor clip of 99.2 MB with system audio), exports compared with FFprobe and with a throwaway that weighs like `SizeBudget.CopyBytes` and makes the real copy beside it.

## Background Exports, Faster AMF, HEVC And AV1 Checked, 13 November 2026

- Exports run on after leaving the editor (`ExportRunner`, one at a time): `Exporting 42%` sits centred in the app bar on every page, a click opens its clip. Another clip's Export waits (`Another clip is exporting`). The clip being exported cannot be deleted, cut or restored meanwhile. Done, a snackbar says `Exported` with Copy File, unless the clip's own editor is open, which shows the Exported panel; a hidden window gets a tray balloon. Export and Save cuts through the editor's player when that clip is open, else directly, retrying while a picture or preview holds the file.
- AMF's `-preanalysis 1` was dropped: over the 60 s clip and an 8.7 s fight at 2.4, 6 and 9 Mbps, VMAF moved by 0.09 at most either way and sizes by under 0.5%, while exports got 1.1 to 1.8 times faster (60 s to Free: 19.7 s to 14.1 s). The rest of an export is decoding (7 s per minute of 1080p60) and Lanczos (1.6 s); a GPU decode saved nothing.
- HEVC and AV1, checked again at the owner's ask: Discord plays HEVC attachments in its desktop and web apps since its patch notes of 1 July 2024; AV1 uploads have no stated support. On this PC Electron's Chromium 156 and WebView2 157 both play HEVC (`hvc1`) and AV1 from AMF at 1080p60, hardware decoded. At the same size HEVC scored 79.2 against H.264's best 72.2 at 2.4 Mbps and 91.6 against 85.8 at 4.8 Mbps (a 20 s Overwatch cut). caniuse puts browser support at 94.75% (18% full, 77% where the device decodes it); every iPhone on iOS 11 or later and nearly every Android phone since 2015 decode it, Firefox since 134 on Windows, 136 on Mac and 137 on Linux; a few Linux desktop apps fail. The owner picked HEVC for every Discord plan.
- How an HEVC export runs: the first card whose HEVC encodes 12 test frames (`hevc_amf`, `hevc_nvenc`, `hevc_qsv`), then the same card's H.264, then x264 two pass; `-profile:v main -tag:v hvc1`, AMF at `-quality quality -rc vbr_peak`, NVENC at `p6`, `hq`, full resolution multipass and spatial AQ, Quick Sync at `slower`, no B frames forced (older cards encode HEVC without them). The output check expects `hevc` from an HEVC encoder.
- Lines for HEVC, a sweep over a 20 s Overwatch cut and an 8.7 s fight at 0.8 to 5 Mbps: 1080p won at every rate (0.8 Mbps 55.8 against 51.6 at 720p and 47.2 at 540p, 1.2 Mbps 63.9, 60.7 and 55.9, 2.4 Mbps 78.6, 75.8 and 70.5, 5 Mbps 92.0, 88.3 and 82.7). AMF's HEVC lands on its bitrate from about 1.5 Mbps at 1080p and overshoots below (0.8 asked came out 1.03, 1.2 came out 1.37; 720p only 7 to 10% over), so `SizeBudget` keeps 1080p down to 0.012 bits per 1080p pixel and frame and 720p down to 0.005.
- In the app, sandbox, first try each time: 60 s of 1080p60 for Free came out HEVC 1920 x 1080 at 18.66 MB and 2.49 Mbps in 29 s (Details said About 18,4 MB and 2,5 Mbps), 5 minutes for Nitro Basic HEVC 1280 x 720 at 47.21 MB and 1.25 Mbps in 73 s (About 46 MB, 1,2 Mbps), 9 s of 1440p144 for Free HEVC 1080p60 at 17.6 MB.

## The Whole Clip Is The File, 14 November 2026

The owner opened a clip, touched nothing, and Size read `About ~~25,7 MB~~ 25,8 MB`. The file is 25,749,743 bytes and its copy weighed 25,751,315, 1,572 bytes more (0.006%), which crossed the rounding line between the two shown digits. Any weight, however close, can do that.

- While the export would be the clip itself, the whole clip with every sound track it has, Size is the file's own size and is never weighed (`ExportPanel.CopyBytesOf`, `CopiesFile`; `copyBytes` in `AppShell.js` reads `data-copy-whole` and `data-copy-file` and does the same during a drag), so it reads once in grey like Bitrate does since that morning. - Checked in a sandbox on a copy of that clip, the three test clips and eight own recordings with one and three tracks: no row reads changed on an untouched clip (the portrait test clip's 605 against 602 KB from 12 November is gone too), and eight moves of Cut Start and Cut End read the same live as rendered, back to `25,7 MB` once and Copy File whenever the selection was whole again.

- Vertical, a row on landscape clips, keeps the tallest 9:16 part of the frame (`ClipExporter.CropSizeOf`, even sizes, 810 x 1440 from 2560 x 1440) where its box sits over the picture, dragged anywhere and remembered with the clip (`ClipMetadata.Crop`, the box's centre as a share of the free width): `crop=W:H:X:0` before the scale, X even. - A hardware encode over its limit gets three tries per encoder (`AttemptsPerEncoder`): the second lowers the bitrate by the miss plus 3%, the third follows the line the first two drew (`NextBitrate`, a secant step, never below half the last try nor above the plain step), since Quick Sync barely shrank on a plain step (51.4 then 50.7 MB for 47.5 MB on the i5). Checked by its Debug self test; no Intel card was at hand on the gaming PC.

## Every Selection Fits And The Video Switch, 17 November 2026
