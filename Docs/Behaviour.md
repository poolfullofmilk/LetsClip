# LetsClip, Looks Wrong, Is Not

Behaviour that looks like a bug or a leftover but is deliberate, each with its reason, so nobody fixes it back. Part of the project memory (`CLAUDE.md`), its rules bind the same way. Add every new one; a dated story behind one lives in `Docs/History.md`.

- Each capture writes two segment streams (`seg_` video, `aud_` audio); the save joins them by their shared timestamps.
- `ClipSaver` writes the clip as `.LetsClip-<guid>.mp4` and renames it only once the remux reports reaching its end with video written (`ClipSaver.WroteVideo`; a second ffprobe read it back until 15 November 2026); such leftovers after a crash are ignored by the library and never deleted, since the clips folder belongs to the user.
- `CacheFolders.Delete` refuses anything but a GUID folder directly under the cache root and only deletes files named like segments or x264 pass logs.
- The preview plays with sound while recording, so its sound lands in the next clip, the owner's pick since clips open unmuted (it used to start muted and warn on unmuting); with Only Record Game and Voice Chat it stays out, since WebView2 plays in LetsClip's own process tree. `letsClip.setVolume` sets the video's `muted` and `volume` itself: a `muted` attribute only mutes an element the HTML parser made, never one Blazor adds, so before v1.3 the preview played sound behind a muted icon.
- An export runs on after leaving the editor (`ExportRunner`), one at a time, with `Exporting 42%` centred in the app bar (`ExportChip`, a click opens its clip, `, 2 Waiting` added while others wait); another clip's Export joins the line (`Waiting for Another Export`, its caption `Another clip is exporting, this one waits its turn`, Cancel takes it out), and a clip exporting or waiting cannot be deleted, cut or restored meanwhile. A finished export says so in a snackbar with Copy File unless its own editor is open (that shows the Exported panel), and in a tray balloon while the window is hidden. Export and Save cuts through the open editor's player when its clip is open, else directly. Exit cancels a running export and waits up to 5 s for it to delete its unfinished file (`Tray.ExitAsync`); before 14 November 2026 it was left to the kill-on-close job like a crash, which left a hidden `.LetsClip-<guid>.mp4` in Exports. A crash still leaves one, never deleted, since the folder is the person's.
- `BlazorWebView.DisposeAsync` disposes its WebView2 after a `ConfigureAwait(false)`, usually on a pool thread, where WebView2's COM calls throw `InvalidCastException` and the controller never closes: every page release from 9 to 12 November failed that way and kept WebView2's memory. The page's template uses `PageWebView`, which moves that dispose back to the UI thread (released in 38 ms, all six WebView2 processes gone, measured 13 November 2026).
- A window capture on the CPU route with AMF or NVENC initialises a second D3D11 device (`d3d11va=spare`) that nothing uses: with two, FFmpeg gives the encoder neither, so it makes its own. Sharing the capture's device crashed AMD's own driver thread (`amdxx64.dll` 32.0.32015.7029, a null read) when the window resized while AMF encoded, the cause of the owner's game window crashes (`Docs/Recovery.md`). On the GPU route the encoder takes the capture's device through its frames either way (the soaked setup), so the spare is left out there since 17 November 2026: 551 handles and 34 MB less. Each device on the 9070 XT holds about 550 handles, most of them events; the 2275 handles the owner's window capture held were three devices, steady over minutes, no leak.
- A stream copy (Save Cut, an Off export) caps each picture's duration at the selection's end (`setts` in `ClipLibrary.CopyRangeAsync`): a still window sends no frames while nothing changes, and the copy kept the last frame's duration from the source, so the owner's cut to 1h9m17s came out 1h10m49s on 17 November 2026. The frames are the same; only the held last one ends on time.
- A sound only export (Video left out) may be shorter than its selection, by the still window's held last frame (`ClipExporter.LengthHolds`); a video export must match within 0.25 s.
- The probe reads a still window recording's frame rate as its nominal 60, not its average (3.7 for the owner's Discord window): the average is taken only while it is at least half the nominal (`MediaProbe`).
- A hover preview starts only when the pointer itself moves (`hover` in `Page.js`): cards scrolling under a still pointer used to start one at every wheel pause, about 100 ms of hitching each.
- A Discord plan export is HEVC (`hvc1` tag, Main profile) on the graphics card: `hevc_amf`, `hevc_nvenc` or `hevc_qsv` after a 12 frame test (`VideoEncoders.HevcOf`, `WorksAsync`), then that card's H.264, then x264 two pass, the owner's pick of 13 November 2026. Off stays an exact copy and capture stays H.264. HEVC scored 6 to 12 VMAF over H.264 at the same size, and 1080p beat 720p and 540p at every bitrate from 0.8 to 5 Mbps, so an encode keeps 1080p down to 0.012 bits per 1080p pixel and frame (1.5 Mbps at 60 FPS, where AMF's HEVC still lands on its bitrate; it overshoots by 14 to 29% below), then down a ladder of 720p, 540p, 360p and 240p at 60 FPS and 240p at 30 and 15, each rung just above where AMF's HEVC stops shrinking, with the sound between 24 and 128 kbps, so Too Long is only a selection past the last rung (`SizeBudget`, `Docs/History.md`, Export: Every Selection Fits And The Video Switch). AMF's HEVC does not start below 384 x 216, so a portrait 240 x 428 export falls to the card's H.264. Discord plays HEVC on desktop and web since 1 July 2024 and about 95% of browsers decode it (caniuse); a device without an HEVC decoder sees a download box, the file still plays in VLC. The progress line still reads `Encoding on AMD AMF`; the log names the codec (`As HEVC With AMD AMF`).
- Replacing a clip (a cut, a restore) retries five times a second apart (`ClipLibrary.ReplaceWhileBusyAsync`): a picture pass or a card's preview reading it lets go in a moment.
- The tray balloon can open another clip into the open editor: each loaded clip has its own lifetime (`_lifetime` token, its own `DotNetObjectReference` let go on detach, its pending trim written when it ends), so a probe or timeline strip still running for the last clip never lands on the next, and `ExportPanel` is keyed by the clip's path, so an export or a pick never carries over.
- A saved clip holds up to a second of keyframe pre-roll below zero behind its MP4 edit list: `ffprobe -show_packets` lists it with negative times and the discard flag, players never show it.
- A lossless cut or Off export of a clip with B frames (`has_b_frames` 2, a file copied in from another recorder, or the x264 test clips) keeps frames past End in the file: a stream copy ends on decode order, and until 17 November 2026 the MP4 edit list showed the frames whose times run past it (470 frames for a 7.8 s cut of a 60 FPS clip against 468, measured on 9 November 2026, its first 468 matching exactly; up to 7 frames for the x264 `veryslow` test clips with 8 B frames, a 54 s cut coming out 54.234 s on 12 November 2026). Since then each picture's duration is capped at the selection's end (`setts`), so the edit list ends on End and those frames stay hidden (an x264 test clip's 20 s cut reads 20.000 s, was 20.117). LetsClip's own recordings never have B frames (`-bf 0`), so their cuts are frame exact, and an export for a Discord plan re-encodes and is exact either way.
- `ExtendedLimits` (`FFmpeg`) and `AdapterDescription` (`MonitorCatalog`) are explicit layouts holding only the fields read, at their 64 bit offsets, like `ModeInfo`; the app is x64 only. Checked after the change: children die with a killed parent and the AMD adapter is still found.
- A capture's FFmpeg is disposed only after its exit code is read (`SegmentProcess.Exited` always completes), so `ReplayRecorder.WatchEndAsync` never waits forever; `GameWatcher` reads and lets go of the found game's handle under one lock.
- A still game window's clip can end its video before its audio: the last frame holds while the sound runs on to the press.

To ..., Up To 388 ms Late`), never from inside the audio lock (`Docs/History.md`, Capture: The Work Laptop's Log).
- A device event restarts a source only when the device it wants changed. The new recorder starts before the old one stops and takes over at its first sound, a second at most (`AudioSource.Connect`, `s_handover`), and a device that will not start leaves the old one recording (`Kept <device>`) until a later event tries again. Sources connect beside the capture's start, so a device slow to open (20 s for the laptop's microphone array once) joins late and its moments stay silent until then.
- Logs are written on NLog's own thread (`WithAsync` with `Grow`): one write on the laptop took a second, and whichever thread logged waited for it, the audio threads among them.
- A display change on another monitor keeps the session: `ddagrab_reopen.patch` opens the lost Desktop Duplication again every 100 ms for up to 30 s, repeating the last frame meanwhile, and `CaptureController.WantedOptions` keeps the running monitor when it only moved (its left, top or primary flag), so a dock, another monitor's rate or a secure desktop never clears the replay or ends a Record. A game or app window keeps its whole running monitor through that monitor's own mode changes too, so a game in exclusive fullscreen at another resolution never restarts on alt tab (since 19 November 2026, read from the code, a real mode change was not tried); a recorded monitor's own mode or index changing still restarts its capture, and its replay is kept for a save.
- Every process LetsClip starts gets an environment without `WEBVIEW2_` variables (`ProcessStarts.WithoutWebView`: FFmpeg, the updater's exe and `cmd`, Explorer), and files and links open through `explorer.exe` (`Explorer.Open`), so they start from Explorer's own environment: LetsClip sets `WEBVIEW2_USER_DATA_FOLDER` for its page, every child inherited it until 18 November 2026, and a WebView2 app started from one would have opened LetsClip's profile.
- The start's update check and the window's first open share one request (`Tray.FindUpdateAsync`), where each asked GitHub on its own.
- A game whose Record switch goes off in Games and Apps lands in `RemovedGames`, and an app's goes off by leaving `AddedGames`; a switch turned back on undoes exactly that, so a removed game never reappears as an added app (`CaptureController.SetRecorded`).
- A segment's bytes live in native memory (`SegmentBuffer`, a `SafeHandle` over `NativeMemory`), never on the .NET heap: each is freed the moment it is evicted or spilled, a save still copying holds it until done (`DangerousAddRef`), and one budget covers every session's segments, a kept replay's included (`SegmentMemory`, the oldest arrivals spilled first). Before 19 November 2026 each second made two large arrays, so half the heap was fragmentation, a full GC ran every 26 s, and a 2 minute Record pushed private memory from 360 to 900 MB for ten minutes.
- A session that ends by itself keeps its replay for its own length (`ReplayRecorder.Keep`): a game closing or crashing, its window rebuilt, an encoder dying, a monitor change or a settings restart. A press then saves it as its own clip, named for when it ended (`Kept From The Ended Session` in the log), beside the running session's clip when that one holds less than the replay length, so one press can give two clips. Lock, sleep, the display turning off and Exit still clear it, and it is saved once. The Save button and the tray's Save stay on while it is kept (`CaptureController.CanSave`).
- The game's window is LetsClip's own pick, not .NET's `MainWindowHandle`: the largest visible, uncloaked, unowned top level window of its process (`GameWatcher.BestWindowOf`), so a hidden, cloaked or zero sized helper window is never recorded as Recording. A window Windows refuses to capture (`Failed to setup graphics capture`, 0x80070057, what a destroyed window gives) is skipped (`GameWatcher.Refuse`) without trying other encoders, and the next window is tried quietly 2 s later (`Trying Another Window Of The Game`); a running game whose window was rebuilt raises `WindowChanged` and records the new one at once. Roblox sat on one refused window for 2.5 minutes on 17 November 2026 before this.
- Presses closer than 2 s apart count as one (`CaptureController.s_pressGap`, each press, an ignored one too, holds the next off), so hammering the key saves one clip; the owner asked for it on 19 November 2026. A press while a save runs still does nothing.
- The Record button and the tray's Record Until Stopped work while a clip saves: a recording pins its own segments, so nothing waits on the save.
- A card's menu is built on its first click (`ClipCard.BuildMenu`, opened in place by `OpenMenuAsync`, anchored exactly as before): a MudMenu with its popover and tooltip on every card was half the cost of drawing a thousand cards.
- Coming back from the editor draws cards up to the opened clip plus one batch (`Clips.Open` caps `s_cards`), never every card drawn before: at 1000 clips a return took 1.5 s and 744 MB of heap before 19 November 2026, 0.1 s and 139 MB now for a recent clip, about 1 s for one 950 cards down.
- Pictures are made by four painters from one line, the cards on screen first (`ClipLibrary.ShowFirst`, `NextPicture`): a thousand copied in clips get their thumbnails in about 12 s, where one at a time took over five minutes.
- Every FFmpeg and ffprobe run closes its redirected pipes (`FFmpeg.Release`, `ReleaseAsync`): .NET leaves pipes read by hand open after `Process.Dispose`, two handles a run that no garbage collection reclaimed (347 handles over a two hour soak, 5,770 after a thousand thumbnails).
- Free space on the cache drive only matters for what spills past the memory budget: a start needs the spill plus 1 GB only when the replay is bigger than the budget, and a running session stops for a nearly full disk only while segments spill to it. A full C: drive no longer stops a one minute replay that lives in memory.
- `LETSCLIP_MEMORY_MB` sets the memory budget, read only in a sandbox (`LETSCLIP_ROOT` set), so a test can make a Record spill in minutes.
- Card changes animate inside the page alone: `letsClip.transition` runs its view transition on `.page-scroll`, or on `.page` in the editor, since a transition over the whole document sends every click for its length to the page's root, and a Record pressed as a saved clip's card arrived did nothing (found by a soak on 19 November 2026). Inside the page a click still lands on its root for those 150 ms, while the cards move.
